How to use it

  1. Tick every allergen you need to avoid. The 14 allergens that EU law requires on food labels are here, plus lactose and carmine (E120).
  2. Pick the language spoken where you are eating. Languages marked “check with staff” have not yet been reviewed by a native speaker.
  3. Print the card or save it as an image on your phone. A paper copy still works when your battery is flat.
  4. Show the card before you order and ask staff to check with the kitchen. If anything is unclear, choose another dish.

What the card says

A short title, the sentence “I have a serious allergy to the following”, your allergens, and a request to keep them out of your food, including traces, shared frying oil and shared utensils. The wording is the allergy card from the IngrediLens app, written language by language; nothing is machine-translated while you use this page.

Frequently asked questions

Is the allergy card really free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no advertising and no tracking. The page runs in your browser and remembers your choices only on this device.

Can I rely on the translation?

The sentences were written carefully, but a card cannot replace a conversation. Watch whether the staff understood, ask them to check with the kitchen, and choose something else if you are unsure. Languages marked “check with staff” are still waiting for a native-speaker review.

Does the card work for coeliac disease?

Tick “Gluten”: the card names wheat, barley and rye and asks about traces, shared oil and shared utensils, which matter for coeliac disease too.

What should I do in an emergency?

The card is not medical advice and does not replace your emergency plan. If someone shows signs of anaphylaxis, use their adrenaline auto-injector if they have one and call emergency services (112 in the EU).
