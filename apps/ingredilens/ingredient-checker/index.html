Ingredient list allergen checker

Paste or type the ingredient list from a food label, tick your allergens, and the checker highlights the ingredients and “may contain” or traces statements that match. It reads labels in English, Spanish, Portuguese, German, French and Italian, and everything happens in your browser.

Not a medical device. The checker compares the text you paste with a list of allergen names; it can miss things and cannot see the real pack. Always read the label on the product and follow your allergist's advice.

How to use it

  1. The text is lowercased and accents are removed, so “Lait écrémé” and “LAIT ECREME” match the same entry. The list is split at commas, brackets, colons and semicolons.
  2. Each part is compared with 848 allergen names and synonyms in six languages from the IngrediLens ingredient database, plus everyday words such as butter, cream and tofu.
  3. Sentences such as “may contain”, “traces of”, “puede contener”, “kann Spuren enthalten”, “peut contenir” or “può contenere tracce” are read separately and shown in amber.
  4. Free-from claims such as “gluten-free” or “sin lactosa” are listed, but they never hide a match found in the ingredients.

What it can miss

  • Unusual names, abbreviations, spelling mistakes and brand names.
  • Allergens the label does not list, or that appear only on another part of the pack.
  • Labels in other languages than the six above.
  • Rules that differ from country to country: the checker does not know where the product was sold.

Frequently asked questions

Does “no matches” mean the product is safe for me?

No. It only means the checker did not find the names it knows for the allergens you chose. Read the whole label, including the allergen statement, and if you are unsure, do not eat the product.

Is my text sent anywhere?

No. The page and its allergen list load once; the check runs in your browser and nothing you paste is sent or stored.

Why are “may contain” warnings shown separately?

They describe possible cross-contact, not a recipe ingredient. Many people with severe allergies avoid these products; follow your allergist's advice.

Which allergens does it cover?

The 14 allergens that EU labels must emphasise (cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, egg, fish, peanuts, soy, milk, tree nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphites, lupin and molluscs), plus lactose and carmine (E120).
