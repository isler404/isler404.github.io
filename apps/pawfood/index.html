Know what's in their bowl

Pawfood · iPhone & iPad

Pawfood is an iPhone app that scans dog and cat food labels and checks the ingredients against your pet's allergies and avoid list. You keep a list for each pet: allergens your vet confirmed, sensitivities you suspect and anything you simply want to avoid.

Download on the App Store

What Pawfood does

Scan any label

Scan a barcode, photograph the ingredient list or pick a photo. Fix any word the camera misread before checking.

Match against each pet

Every scan is checked against all of your pets at once, each with their own species, life stage and avoid list.

Explain vague terms

"Animal fat", "poultry" or "natural flavor" don't name a species. Pawfood marks them as worth a closer look instead of guessing.

Compare on dry matter

Wet and dry foods only compare fairly once water is removed. Pawfood converts the guaranteed analysis for you.

Keep a food journal

Log meals, treats, weight and what you observe, and export a PDF summary to bring to your veterinarian.

Brand-level recall notices

Optional lookups of public recall records (openFDA) for the brand you scanned.

How it works

  1. Add your dog or cat and the ingredients or allergen families to watch, marked as vet-confirmed, suspected or just avoiding.
  2. Scan the barcode or the ingredient list. Text is read on the device.
  3. Pawfood splits the list into ingredients and matches each one, including other names and other languages ("chicken meal", "Hühnermehl", "tavuk unu"), against its knowledge base of 767 ingredients and 76 allergen families.
  4. You get one of four results per pet, with the ingredient, the item on your list and the reason for every match.

Four possible results

Each result has a title and a symbol, never color alone.

Too little of the label could be read or recognized (under 60% of the ingredients), so Pawfood does not give a result. Scan again or correct the text.

Your data stays on your device

Pets, avoid lists, scans and journal entries are stored only on your iPhone or iPad. There is no account, no ads and no tracking. If you buy or restore Pawfood Plus, an anonymous purchase record (product, price, dates) goes to RevenueCat under a random ID, never your name, Apple Account or pet data. If you turn on online lookups, only a barcode number goes to Open Pet Food Facts and a brand name to openFDA, never anything about you or your pet.

Questions and answers

Does "No recorded match" mean the food is fine for my pet?

No. It only means nothing on the reported ingredient list matched the items you saved. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and cross-contact from shared equipment is not listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check the food with your vet.

Why is "poultry fat" flagged when my dog is allergic to chicken?

"Poultry" can be chicken, turkey, duck or other birds, and the label doesn't say which. Pawfood shows it as worth a closer look so you can ask the manufacturer. Rendered fats usually carry less protein than meat, but the amount is not stated on the label.

Can Pawfood tell me what my pet is allergic to?

No. Pawfood doesn't diagnose, treat or prescribe diets. Food allergies are usually worked out with your veterinarian through an elimination diet. Pawfood helps you read labels and keep notes for that conversation.

Does it work for cats as well as dogs?

Yes. Each pet has its own species, and some notes are species-specific, for example propylene glycol in cat food or the note when a cat food lists no added taurine.

Which languages and labels does it read?

The app is in English, Turkish, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, German and French, and it recognizes ingredient names in those languages plus common Italian and Dutch names.

Do I need an account or internet connection?

No account. Scanning and matching work offline. Barcode lookups and recall searches are optional and can be turned off in Settings.

Where does the ingredient information come from?

From public sources such as AAFCO, FEDIAF, EU feed law, the U.S. FDA, WSAVA, the Merck Veterinary Manual and peer-reviewed papers, cited in the app under Sources & methods. Product data comes from Open Pet Food Facts (ODbL).
