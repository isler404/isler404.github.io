Guides

Plain-language guides to reading dog and cat food labels. They explain what the label can and cannot tell you; questions about your own pet's health belong with your veterinarian.

  • Food allergy or food intolerance? How they differ in dogs and catsFood allergy involves the immune system; intolerance doesn't. Signs overlap, so only a vet-led elimination trial can tell them apart. What to know and note.
  • \"Poultry\", \"animal fat\", \"natural flavor\": vague pet food label terms explainedWhich pet food label terms don't name the animal, what each one can contain, why it matters for allergy lists, and how to get a straight answer from the manufacturer.
  • What "by-product" and "meal" really mean on a pet food labelBy-products and meals are defined feed terms, not insults. Learn what they include, why the species name matters and how to read them for allergies.
  • Most commonly reported food allergens in cats, and where they hide on labelsBeef, fish and chicken top the published cat cases. What the numbers mean, why they aren't a risk ranking, and where those proteins hide on cat food labels.
  • Dry matter basis explained: how to compare wet and dry pet foodWhat dry matter is, how to convert a guaranteed analysis step by step, how to estimate carbohydrates honestly, and the mistakes that make comparisons misleading.
  • Keeping a pet food journal: what to record and how to share it with your vetA practical guide to logging foods, treats, symptoms, stool and weight in a way your vet can use, without reading too much into the patterns yourself.
  • Grain-free pet food: what the label means and what it doesn'tGrain-free means no cereal grains; it isn't a low-carb or allergy claim. What replaces grains, and what the FDA said about diet and DCM.
  • How to read a dog or cat food label, step by stepA practical walk through every part of a pet food label: product name rules, ingredient order, guaranteed analysis, the adequacy statement, feeding guide and the small print.
  • Hydrolyzed protein diets: how they work and what the label tells youHydrolysis breaks proteins into small pieces the immune system is less likely to recognize. Less likely is not never: what studies found, what to ask.
  • Preservatives in pet food: mixed tocopherols, BHA, BHT and ethoxyquinA neutral look at why pet foods contain preservatives, how natural and synthetic antioxidants differ, what the rules say and how to read them on a label.
  • Puppy and kitten food labels: growth, all life stages and large breedsWhat to check on a puppy or kitten food label, from the growth statement and the large-breed wording to calories, calcium and feeding guides.
  • Senior pet food labels: what "senior" does and doesn't tell youThere is no separate legal standard for senior pet food. Learn what to check on the label instead, from calories and protein to joint claims and missing values.
  • How treats and toppers can undo an allergy dietTreats, chews, toppers and flavoured extras often carry the very proteins a diet avoids. Here is how to check them and keep a diet trial consistent.
  • Wet vs dry pet food: how to compare the labels fairlyWet food looks low in protein because it is mostly water. Learn to compare canned and dry labels on a dry matter and calorie basis, step by step.
