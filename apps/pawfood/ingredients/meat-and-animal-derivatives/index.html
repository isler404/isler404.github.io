Meat and animal derivatives

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: EU category term for meat and animal by-products whose species are not named unless declared, for example "of which chicken 4%".

What it is

"By-products" are the parts of the animal other than muscle meat, such as organs, blood and other clean tissue. They are a regulated feed term, not a quality grade, and they carry the protein of the species they come from.

On the label it is a protein source.

"Meat and animal derivatives" is a category name allowed on EU labels. It groups several animal materials under one heading and does not have to name the species, though some labels add a share such as "(of which chicken 4%)". A named share tells you one species is present, not that it is the only one.

For a pet with an allergy list, a category name like this cannot be checked from the label. Pawfood marks it as worth a closer look for every mammal and poultry family it could include.

Allergen family

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Beef, Pork, Lamb / sheep, Chicken, Turkey, Duck, Goat, Venison (deer), Rabbit, Horse and Poultry (any).

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

High. This ingredient is expected to carry the protein of its source in full. If the source is on your pet's list, it matters as much as the meat itself.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for meat and animal derivatives. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

Because the species is not named, a pet with Beef, Pork, Lamb / sheep, Chicken, Turkey, Duck, Goat, Venison (deer), Rabbit, Horse and Poultry (any) on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
