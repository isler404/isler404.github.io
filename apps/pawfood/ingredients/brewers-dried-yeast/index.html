Brewers dried yeast

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Dried yeast from brewing, a source of protein and B vitamins; may carry traces of barley.

What it is

Dried, dehydrated or freeze-dried forms have the water removed without rendering. Like a meal, they are concentrated, so a small share of the recipe can still carry a lot of protein.

On the label it is a protein source.

Brewers dried yeast is a by-product of brewing. It adds B vitamins, protein and flavor. Some owners avoid yeast; a yeast sensitivity is different from a yeast skin infection, and both are topics for the vet.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts brewers dried yeast in the Yeast family.

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Barley.

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

High. This ingredient is expected to carry the protein of its source in full. If the source is on your pet's list, it matters as much as the meat itself.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for brewers dried yeast. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Yeast saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, brewers dried yeast gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

Because the species is not named, a pet with Barley on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
