Green-lipped mussel

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Dried New Zealand green-lipped mussel, a source of omega-3 fatty acids and glycosaminoglycans that carries shellfish protein.

What it is

Dried, dehydrated or freeze-dried forms have the water removed without rendering. Like a meal, they are concentrated, so a small share of the recipe can still carry a lot of protein.

On the label it is a supplement.

Green-lipped mussel is a mollusc used as a joint-support ingredient. It is shellfish, which matters for a pet with shellfish on its list.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts green-lipped mussel in the Shellfish (crustaceans, molluscs) family.

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

High. This ingredient is expected to carry the protein of its source in full. If the source is on your pet's list, it matters as much as the meat itself.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for green-lipped mussel. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Shellfish (crustaceans, molluscs) saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, green-lipped mussel gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
