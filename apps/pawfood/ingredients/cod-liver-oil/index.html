Cod liver oil

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Oil from cod liver containing vitamins A and D and omega-3 fatty acids; protein content is low but not zero.

What it is

Oils are pressed or extracted and then refined to different degrees. Refined oils contain very little protein, though trace amounts are possible and the degree of refining is not on the label.

On the label it is a fat source, for energy and essential fatty acids.

Cod liver oil is rich in vitamins A and D as well as omega-3s, so it contributes to the food's vitamin levels. Extra supplementation on top of a complete food is something to discuss with your vet.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts cod liver oil in the Cod, Whitefish (white-fleshed fish) and Fish (any) family.

Fish (any) is among the food allergen sources most often reported in dogs and cats (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016).

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

Reduced. Processing lowers the amount of intact protein (for example rendering a fat or hydrolyzing a protein), but does not remove it reliably. Whether a reduced amount matters for your pet is a question for your veterinarian.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for cod liver oil. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Cod, Whitefish (white-fleshed fish) and Fish (any) saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, cod liver oil gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

For a vet-confirmed allergy Pawfood adds a note that this form carries less protein. The result itself is not lowered.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
