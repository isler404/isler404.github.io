Natural flavor

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Non-specific flavouring that may be derived from animal or plant sources.

What it is

Flavors are added for palatability. A named flavor must come mostly from the named source, but the label does not say how it was made, and flavors can be made from digests that carry protein.

On the label it is a flavor or palatability enhancer.

"Natural flavor" means the flavor comes from natural sources rather than chemical synthesis. It does not say which source. In pet food it is often an animal digest, which carries protein from an unnamed animal.

That is why Pawfood marks plain "natural flavor" as worth a closer look for pets with an animal protein on their list. A named flavor ("natural chicken flavor") is matched directly.

Allergen family

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Chicken, Turkey, Duck, Poultry (any), Beef, Pork, Lamb / sheep, Fish (any), Salmon, Tuna, Dairy (milk), Egg, Yeast, Wheat, Soy and Corn (maize).

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

Unknown. The label term does not say enough about the source or processing to estimate how much protein it carries.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for natural flavor. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

Because the species is not named, a pet with Chicken, Turkey, Duck, Poultry (any), Beef, Pork, Lamb / sheep, Fish (any), Salmon, Tuna, Dairy (milk), Egg, Yeast, Wheat, Soy and Corn (maize) on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
