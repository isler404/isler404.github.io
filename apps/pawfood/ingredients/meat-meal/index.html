Meat meal

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Rendered meal from mammal tissue whose species is not stated; AAFCO excludes poultry from this term.

What it is

A "meal" is a rendered ingredient: the raw material is cooked, the fat is separated and the rest is dried and ground into a concentrated powder. Because most of the water is gone, a meal supplies more protein per gram than the same fresh meat.

On the label it is a protein source.

Meat meal (also meat and bone meal) is rendered mammal tissue. The species is not named and can vary between batches, so it is a possible match for beef, pork, lamb and other mammals.

Allergen family

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Beef, Pork, Lamb / sheep, Goat, Horse and Venison (deer).

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

High. This ingredient is expected to carry the protein of its source in full. If the source is on your pet's list, it matters as much as the meat itself.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for meat meal. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

Because the species is not named, a pet with Beef, Pork, Lamb / sheep, Goat, Horse and Venison (deer) on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
