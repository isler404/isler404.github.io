Hydrolyzed fish protein

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Fish protein broken down into smaller peptides; the degree of hydrolysis varies by product; the exact species is not stated on the label.

What it is

Hydrolysis breaks proteins into smaller pieces (peptides) with water and enzymes or heat. Smaller pieces are less likely to be recognized by the immune system, which is why hydrolyzed diets exist, but the ingredient still comes from the same species, and how far the protein is broken down varies.

On the label it is a protein source.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts hydrolyzed fish protein in the Fish (any) family.

Fish (any) is among the food allergen sources most often reported in dogs and cats (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016).

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Salmon, Trout, Whitefish (white-fleshed fish), Cod, Pollock / saithe, Haddock, Hake, Whiting, Flatfish (plaice, sole, flounder), Hoki, Tilapia, Catfish, Tuna, Mackerel, Sardine / pilchard, Herring, Anchovy, Menhaden, Sprat and Capelin.

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

Reduced. Processing lowers the amount of intact protein (for example rendering a fat or hydrolyzing a protein), but does not remove it reliably. Whether a reduced amount matters for your pet is a question for your veterinarian.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for hydrolyzed fish protein. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Fish (any) saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, hydrolyzed fish protein gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

For a vet-confirmed allergy Pawfood adds a note that this form carries less protein. The result itself is not lowered.

Because the species is not named, a pet with Salmon, Trout, Whitefish (white-fleshed fish), Cod, Pollock / saithe, Haddock, Hake, Whiting, Flatfish (plaice, sole, flounder), Hoki, Tilapia, Catfish, Tuna, Mackerel, Sardine / pilchard, Herring, Anchovy, Menhaden, Sprat and Capelin on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
