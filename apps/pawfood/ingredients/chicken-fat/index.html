Chicken fat

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Rendered chicken fat; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content.

What it is

Rendered fat is separated from the protein part of the tissue during rendering. It is mostly fat, but it is not purified, so small amounts of protein can remain. The label never states how much.

On the label it is a fat source, for energy and essential fatty acids.

Chicken fat is a common fat source and palatant in kibble, including in foods whose main protein is not chicken. That is why it often surprises owners who chose, say, a lamb or fish recipe.

Rendered chicken fat carries much less protein than chicken meat, but it is not protein-free. Some veterinarians ask owners to avoid every chicken-derived ingredient during an elimination trial; others accept refined fats. Ask your vet which applies to your pet.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts chicken fat in the Chicken and Poultry (any) family.

Chicken is among the food allergen sources most often reported in dogs and cats (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016).

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

Reduced. Processing lowers the amount of intact protein (for example rendering a fat or hydrolyzing a protein), but does not remove it reliably. Whether a reduced amount matters for your pet is a question for your veterinarian.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for chicken fat. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Chicken and Poultry (any) saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, chicken fat gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

For a vet-confirmed allergy Pawfood adds a note that this form carries less protein. The result itself is not lowered.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
