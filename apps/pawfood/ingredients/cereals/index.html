Cereals

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Non-specific cereal grains; the label does not say which grains are used.

What it is

On a label, a named meat or fish without a qualifier ("chicken", "salmon") usually means fresh or frozen flesh. Fresh meat is roughly 70% water, and ingredients are listed by weight before cooking, so a whole meat at the top of the list can weigh much less once the food is dried.

On the label it is a carbohydrate (energy) source.

"Cereals" is an EU category name that can cover wheat, maize, rice, barley, oats and others without naming them. For a pet with a grain on its list, the label alone cannot answer which grain is inside.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts cereals in the Cereal grains (any) family.

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Wheat, Corn (maize), Rice, Barley, Oats, Rye, Sorghum, Millet and Gluten grains (wheat, barley, rye).

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

High. This ingredient is expected to carry the protein of its source in full. If the source is on your pet's list, it matters as much as the meat itself.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for cereals. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Cereal grains (any) saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, cereals gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

Because the species is not named, a pet with Wheat, Corn (maize), Rice, Barley, Oats, Rye, Sorghum, Millet and Gluten grains (wheat, barley, rye) on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
