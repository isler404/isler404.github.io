Fish by-products

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Non-muscle fish parts from species that are not stated.

What it is

"By-products" are the parts of the animal other than muscle meat, such as organs, blood and other clean tissue. They are a regulated feed term, not a quality grade, and they carry the protein of the species they come from.

On the label it is a protein source.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts fish by-products in the Fish (any) family.

Fish (any) is among the food allergen sources most often reported in dogs and cats (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016).

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Salmon, Tuna, Whitefish (white-fleshed fish), Cod, Pollock / saithe, Herring, Sardine / pilchard, Anchovy and Mackerel.

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

High. This ingredient is expected to carry the protein of its source in full. If the source is on your pet's list, it matters as much as the meat itself.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for fish by-products. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Fish (any) saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, fish by-products gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

Because the species is not named, a pet with Salmon, Tuna, Whitefish (white-fleshed fish), Cod, Pollock / saithe, Herring, Sardine / pilchard, Anchovy and Mackerel on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
