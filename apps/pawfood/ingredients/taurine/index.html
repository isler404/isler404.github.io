Taurine

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: An amino sulfonic acid that is essential in the diet of cats.

What it is

On the label it is an added amino acid.

Taurine is an amino acid that cats cannot make in sufficient amounts, so it must come from the diet. Taurine deficiency in cats has been linked to heart disease (DCM) and retinal degeneration, which is why it is added to complete cat foods. Meat and fish contain some taurine naturally, and cooking reduces it.

Dogs normally make taurine from other amino acids. It has been discussed in connection with the U.S. FDA's DCM investigation in dogs; questions about a particular dog belong with the veterinarian.

Allergen family

Pawfood does not place taurine in an allergen family. You can still add it to a pet's list as an individual ingredient to avoid.

Dogs and cats

For Cats, when a recognized ingredient list has no added taurine, Pawfood shows this note: “Taurine is essential for cats. It is not listed as an added ingredient; meat and fish supply some, so check that the food is labelled complete and balanced for cats.” (information only).

Questions about your pet's health? Ask your vet.

How Pawfood flags it

It is flagged only if you add taurine itself to a pet's list.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
