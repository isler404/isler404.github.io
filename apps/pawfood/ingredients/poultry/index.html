Poultry

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Poultry meat or flesh, which carries poultry protein; the exact species is not stated on the label.

What it is

On a label, a named meat or fish without a qualifier ("chicken", "salmon") usually means fresh or frozen flesh. Fresh meat is roughly 70% water, and ingredients are listed by weight before cooking, so a whole meat at the top of the list can weigh much less once the food is dried.

On the label it is a protein source.

"Poultry" is a collective term: it can be chicken, turkey, duck or other domesticated birds, and the mix can change between batches. The label does not have to say which bird was used.

If your pet has a poultry species on its list, the only way to know is to ask the manufacturer which species the ingredient contains. See the guide on vague label terms.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts poultry in the Poultry (any) family.

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Chicken, Turkey, Duck, Goose, Quail, Pheasant and Guinea fowl.

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

High. This ingredient is expected to carry the protein of its source in full. If the source is on your pet's list, it matters as much as the meat itself.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for poultry. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Poultry (any) saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, poultry gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

Because the species is not named, a pet with Chicken, Turkey, Duck, Goose, Quail, Pheasant and Guinea fowl on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
