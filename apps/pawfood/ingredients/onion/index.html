Onion

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Onion or onion powder, an Allium vegetable.

What it is

On a label, a named meat or fish without a qualifier ("chicken", "salmon") usually means fresh or frozen flesh. Fresh meat is roughly 70% water, and ingredients are listed by weight before cooking, so a whole meat at the top of the list can weigh much less once the food is dried.

On the label it is a flavor or palatability enhancer.

Onion and related plants (leek, chives, shallot) can damage red blood cells in dogs and cats, and cooking or drying does not remove the effect. Onion powder in a pet food or human food is shown as an alert. If your pet ate onion, contact your vet.

Allergen family

Pawfood does not place onion in an allergen family. You can still add it to a pet's list as an individual ingredient to avoid.

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

Minimal. Highly refined or purified, so little protein is expected to remain. Trace amounts cannot be excluded from the label alone.

Dogs and cats

For dogs and cats, Pawfood shows this note: “Onion and related Allium plants can damage red blood cells in dogs and cats; cats are more sensitive.” (the result becomes Match found).

Questions about your pet's health? Ask your vet.

How Pawfood flags it

It is flagged only if you add onion itself to a pet's list.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
