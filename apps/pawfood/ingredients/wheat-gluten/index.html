Wheat gluten

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Concentrated wheat protein (gluten).

What it is

Isolates and concentrates are protein fractions separated from the rest of the material. They exist to add protein, so they carry the source's protein in concentrated form.

On the label it is a protein source.

Wheat gluten is the protein fraction of wheat. It binds chunks in wet food and raises protein in kibble. It is a wheat ingredient for matching purposes and is not a meat protein, even though it adds to the crude protein number.

Allergen family

Pawfood counts wheat gluten in the Wheat, Gluten grains (wheat, barley, rye) and Cereal grains (any) family.

Wheat is among the food allergen sources most often reported in dogs and cats (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016).

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

High. This ingredient is expected to carry the protein of its source in full. If the source is on your pet's list, it matters as much as the meat itself.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for wheat gluten. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

If a pet's list has Wheat, Gluten grains (wheat, barley, rye) and Cereal grains (any) saved as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you avoid, wheat gluten gives Match found. Saved as a suspected sensitivity, it gives Worth a closer look.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
