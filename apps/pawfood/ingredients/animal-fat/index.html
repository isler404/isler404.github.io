Animal fat

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In short: Rendered fat from animal species that are not stated; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content.

What it is

Rendered fat is separated from the protein part of the tissue during rendering. It is mostly fat, but it is not purified, so small amounts of protein can remain. The label never states how much.

On the label it is a fat source, for energy and essential fatty acids.

"Animal fat" is rendered fat from unnamed animals, often a mix. It may be preserved with mixed tocopherols or a synthetic antioxidant, which the label may show in brackets.

It is one of the label terms Pawfood marks as worth a closer look for pets with a meat or poultry family on their list, because there is no way to tell the species from the label.

Allergen family

The label term does not name the species, so it could come from: Beef, Pork, Lamb / sheep, Chicken, Poultry (any) and Fish (any).

Protein carryover

Protein carryover describes how much of the source's protein an ingredient is expected to carry into the food. Food allergies in dogs and cats are reactions to proteins, so the same species can matter more in one form than another.

Reduced. Processing lowers the amount of intact protein (for example rendering a fat or hydrolyzing a protein), but does not remove it reliably. Whether a reduced amount matters for your pet is a question for your veterinarian.

Dogs and cats

Pawfood has no species-specific rule for animal fat. For questions about your own pet's diet, your veterinarian is the right person to ask.

How Pawfood flags it

Because the species is not named, a pet with Beef, Pork, Lamb / sheep, Chicken, Poultry (any) and Fish (any) on its list gets Worth a closer look rather than a firm match. Pawfood does not guess.

This doesn't mean the food is allergen-free. Labels can be incomplete, recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet has a diagnosed allergy, check with your vet.
