Chicken and other poultry

  • ChickenChicken meat or flesh, which carries chicken protein.
  • Chicken mealRendered and dried chicken tissue concentrated into a protein meal.
  • Chicken by-product mealRendered meal from chicken parts other than muscle meat, such as organs and frames.
  • Chicken by-productsNon-muscle chicken parts such as organs, used fresh or frozen.
  • Chicken fatRendered chicken fat; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content.
  • Hydrolyzed chicken proteinChicken protein broken down into smaller peptides; the degree of hydrolysis varies by product.
  • Hydrolyzed chicken liverChicken liver broken down by hydrolysis, often used as a palatability enhancer.
  • Chicken liverChicken liver, an organ meat that carries chicken protein.
  • Chicken brothLiquid made by simmering chicken, which contains soluble chicken protein.
  • Chicken digestChicken tissue broken down by enzymes or heat, commonly used as a palatability enhancer.
  • Natural chicken flavorFlavouring derived from chicken, which may contain small amounts of chicken protein.
  • TurkeyTurkey meat or flesh, which carries turkey protein.
  • Turkey mealRendered and dried turkey tissue concentrated into a protein meal.
  • DuckDuck meat or flesh, which carries duck protein.
  • PoultryPoultry meat or flesh, which carries poultry protein; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Poultry mealRendered and dried poultry tissue concentrated into a protein meal; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Poultry by-product mealRendered meal from poultry parts other than muscle meat, such as organs and frames; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Poultry fatRendered poultry fat; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Hydrolyzed poultry proteinPoultry protein broken down into smaller peptides; the degree of hydrolysis varies by product; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Poultry digestPoultry tissue broken down by enzymes or heat, commonly used as a palatability enhancer; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Natural poultry flavorFlavouring derived from poultry, which may contain small amounts of poultry protein; the exact species is not stated on the label.

  • Beef mealRendered and dried beef tissue concentrated into a protein meal.
  • Beef fatRendered beef fat; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content.
  • Beef liverBeef liver, an organ meat that carries beef protein.

  • Pork mealRendered and dried pork tissue concentrated into a protein meal.
  • RabbitRabbit meat or flesh, which carries rabbit protein.
  • KangarooKangaroo meat or flesh, which carries kangaroo protein.

Fish and seafood

  • FishFish meat or flesh, which carries fish protein; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Fish mealRendered and dried fish tissue concentrated into a protein meal; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Fish oilOil extracted from fish; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Hydrolyzed fish proteinFish protein broken down into smaller peptides; the degree of hydrolysis varies by product; the exact species is not stated on the label.
  • Fish by-productsNon-muscle fish parts from species that are not stated.
  • Ocean fishFish from the sea whose species is not stated.
  • SalmonSalmon meat or flesh, which carries salmon protein.
  • Salmon mealRendered and dried salmon tissue concentrated into a protein meal.
  • Salmon oilOil extracted from salmon; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content.
  • Hydrolyzed salmon proteinSalmon protein broken down into smaller peptides; the degree of hydrolysis varies by product.
  • TunaTuna meat or flesh, which carries tuna protein.
  • WhitefishWhitefish meat or flesh, which carries whitefish protein.
  • Menhaden mealRendered and dried menhaden tissue concentrated into a protein meal.
  • Cod liver oilOil from cod liver containing vitamins A and D and omega-3 fatty acids; protein content is low but not zero.
  • Krill oilOil extracted from krill; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content.
  • Green-lipped musselDried New Zealand green-lipped mussel, a source of omega-3 fatty acids and glycosaminoglycans that carries shellfish protein.

Unnamed animal sources

  • MeatNon-specific meat from one or more animal species that the label does not name.
  • Meat and animal derivativesEU category term for meat and animal by-products whose species are not named unless declared, for example "of which chicken 4%".
  • Meat mealRendered meal from mammal tissue whose species is not stated; AAFCO excludes poultry from this term.
  • Meat by-productsNon-muscle mammal parts such as organs from species that are not stated; AAFCO excludes poultry from this term.
  • Meat by-product mealRendered meal from animal parts other than muscle meat, from species that are not stated.
  • Animal proteinNon-specific animal protein whose source species is not stated.
  • Hydrolyzed animal proteinAnimal protein broken into smaller peptides whose source species is not stated.
  • Animal fatRendered fat from animal species that are not stated; mostly lipid, with lower but not zero protein content.
  • TallowRendered fat usually from cattle or sheep; species is not always stated.
  • Animal digestAnimal tissue broken down by enzymes or heat and used for palatability; source species is often not stated.
  • LiverLiver whose source species is not stated on the label.
  • Animal plasmaDried blood plasma whose source species is not stated.
  • Blood mealDried blood whose source species is often not stated.
  • GelatinProcessed collagen used for texture; source species is often not stated.
  • CollagenCollagen protein, often hydrolyzed; source species is often not stated.
  • Bone brothLiquid made by simmering bones whose species is not stated.
  • BrothCooking liquid from animal tissue whose species is not stated.
  • Bone mealGround bone used mainly as a calcium and phosphorus source; species is often not stated.

Egg, dairy and insects

  • EggWhole hen's egg, which carries egg white and yolk proteins.
  • Dried egg productDried whole egg or egg parts, which carries egg proteins.
  • Eggshell membraneThe inner membrane of the eggshell, a source of collagen and glucosamine that may carry egg protein.
  • MilkCow's milk, which carries milk proteins such as casein and whey proteins.
  • WheyThe liquid part of milk left after cheesemaking, which carries whey proteins.
  • CaseinThe main protein of milk, sometimes added as caseinate.
  • CheeseCheese made from milk, which carries milk proteins.
  • LactoseMilk sugar; purified lactose carries little milk protein, but traces are possible.
  • Black soldier fly larvae mealDried and ground black soldier fly larvae, an insect protein meal.

Grains

  • WheatWheat grain, which carries wheat proteins including gluten.
  • Wheat flourMilled wheat, which carries wheat proteins including gluten.
  • Wheat glutenConcentrated wheat protein (gluten).
  • CornCorn (maize) grain, which carries corn proteins.
  • Corn mealGround corn, which carries corn proteins.
  • Corn gluten mealConcentrated corn protein left after starch extraction; corn "gluten" is not wheat gluten.
  • RiceRice grain, which carries rice proteins.
  • Brewers riceBroken rice fragments left from rice milling.
  • Brown riceWhole-grain rice with the bran layer, which carries rice proteins.
  • Rice branOuter layer of the rice grain, used for fiber and fat.
  • BarleyBarley grain, which carries barley proteins including gluten-type proteins.
  • OatsOat grain; oats may be cross-contaminated with gluten grains unless certified.
  • SorghumSorghum grain, a gluten-free cereal.
  • CerealsNon-specific cereal grains; the label does not say which grains are used.

Legumes, potatoes and other starches

  • PeasPeas, a pulse that carries pea proteins.
  • Pea proteinConcentrated pea protein.
  • Pea starchStarch extracted from peas; some pea protein can remain.
  • Pea fiberFiber from pea hulls; some pea protein can remain.
  • LentilsLentils, a pulse that carries lentil proteins.
  • ChickpeasChickpeas, a pulse that carries chickpea proteins.
  • Soybean mealGround soybeans left after oil extraction, a concentrated soy protein source.
  • Soy protein isolateConcentrated soy protein.
  • Hydrolyzed soy proteinSoy protein broken down into smaller peptides; used in some veterinary hydrolyzed diets.
  • PotatoPotato, used as a carbohydrate source.
  • Sweet potatoSweet potato, used as a carbohydrate and fiber source.
  • TapiocaStarch from cassava root; protein content is low.
  • AlfalfaDried alfalfa (lucerne) leaves and stems, a legume forage used for fiber and nutrients.

Plant oils and fibers

  • Sunflower oilOil pressed from sunflower seeds, a source of omega-6 linoleic acid.
  • Canola oilOil from canola (low-erucic rapeseed), a source of omega-6 and omega-3 fatty acids.
  • FlaxseedFlaxseed, a source of fiber and omega-3 alpha-linolenic acid.
  • Vegetable oilPlant oil whose source plant is not stated; refined oils carry very little protein.
  • Algal oilOil from microalgae, a source of DHA omega-3.
  • Powdered cellulosePurified plant cellulose used as insoluble fiber.
  • Beet pulpFiber left after sugar extraction from sugar beets.
  • FructooligosaccharidesShort-chain fructose fibers used as a prebiotic.
  • Psyllium huskHusk of psyllium seeds, a soluble fiber.

Amino acids and supplements

  • TaurineAn amino sulfonic acid that is essential in the diet of cats.
  • DL-methionineA source of the essential amino acid methionine.
  • L-carnitineA nutrient involved in fat metabolism, added as a supplement.
  • GlucosamineA joint supplement usually made from shellfish shells or by fermentation; the source is rarely stated.
  • Chondroitin sulfateA joint supplement extracted from animal cartilage whose species is rarely stated.
  • Choline chlorideA source of choline, an essential nutrient.
  • Menadione nicotinamide bisulfiteA synthetic source of vitamin K activity.
  • Alpha-lipoic acidAn antioxidant used in some supplements and foods.
  • Brewers dried yeastDried yeast from brewing, a source of protein and B vitamins; may carry traces of barley.
  • Yeast extractSoluble yeast contents used for flavour and nutrients.
  • Dried fermentation productsDried microbial fermentation products whose organisms or media are not stated.
  • Yucca schidigera extractExtract of the Yucca schidigera plant, used to reduce stool odour.

Preservatives and antioxidants

  • Mixed tocopherolsNatural vitamin E compounds used to keep fats from going rancid, often from vegetable oils.
  • Rosemary extractRosemary extract used as a natural antioxidant.
  • Citric acidAn acid used to preserve fats and adjust acidity.
  • BHAButylated hydroxyanisole, a synthetic antioxidant preservative.
  • BHTButylated hydroxytoluene, a synthetic antioxidant preservative.
  • EthoxyquinA synthetic antioxidant preservative, also used in fish meal.
  • TBHQTertiary butylhydroquinone, a synthetic antioxidant preservative.
  • Potassium sorbateA mould inhibitor used in moist foods and treats.
  • Sodium benzoateBenzoic acid or its salt, a preservative to which cats are sensitive.
  • Propylene glycolA humectant used in some semi-moist dog foods and treats.

Flavors, colors and thickeners

  • Natural flavorNon-specific flavouring that may be derived from animal or plant sources.
  • Hydrolyzed vegetable proteinPlant protein broken down into a savory flavouring; the plant is not always stated.
  • CarrageenanA thickener extracted from red seaweed, common in wet foods.
  • Guar gumA thickener from guar beans.
  • Caramel colorCaramelized sugar used as a brown colour.
  • Titanium dioxideA white mineral pigment used as a colour.
  • Red 40A synthetic red food dye.

Plants with species notes

  • GarlicGarlic or garlic powder, an Allium plant.
  • OnionOnion or onion powder, an Allium vegetable.
  • GrapesGrapes or grape by-products.
  • CocoaCocoa or chocolate, which contains theobromine and caffeine.
  • HopsHop flowers, used in brewing.
  • Macadamia nutsMacadamia nuts, a tree nut.
  • XylitolA sugar alcohol sweetener.
  • PumpkinPumpkin or winter squash, used as a vegetable and fiber source.
