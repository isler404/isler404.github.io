What Pawfood is

Pawfood helps you read pet food labels. It compares the ingredients it reads or finds against the items you saved for your pet and explains what it found, including what it could not read or could not identify.

What Pawfood is not

  • Pawfood is not a veterinarian and does not provide veterinary advice. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition and does not prescribe diets.
  • Your veterinarian's instructions always come first. Talk to your vet before changing your pet's diet, especially for allergies, illness, pregnancy, growth or prescription diets.
  • If your pet shows signs of a serious reaction (for example trouble breathing, swelling, collapse, repeated vomiting), contact a veterinarian or emergency clinic right away.

Results and their limits

  • "No recorded match" means that nothing in the ingredient list matched what you saved. It does not mean a food is safe, suitable or free of any allergen.
  • Labels can be incomplete or out of date, formulas change, and cross-contact during manufacturing is not shown on labels.
  • Text recognition can misread words. Check the result against the package in your hand.
  • Catalog data may come from public sources such as Open Pet Food Facts, from manufacturers or from other users. It can be incomplete, outdated or wrong.
  • Recall information comes from public sources and is shown at brand level. Pawfood only says a specific package is affected when the lot or barcode matches exactly. Always check the official notice.
  • Ingredient explanations are written by our team from published sources. They are reviewed over time; the review status is shown in the app.

Your data
