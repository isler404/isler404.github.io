Turkey allergy in dogs and cats: what to check on the label

Last updated 7 November 2026 · By Pawfood

If your vet has confirmed a turkey allergy in your dog or cat, or you simply avoid turkey, the ingredients list is where you check. This page lists the label names that always mean turkey, the vaguer terms that might, and how Pawfood flags both.

What counts as turkey

Turkey covers the meat, organs, skin, necks, fat and broth of the domestic turkey, and meals or hydrolysates made from them. It is often paired with chicken or used as a "poultry" ingredient, so a turkey-led recipe isn't necessarily free of other birds.

Terms that might mean turkey

These wordings can contain turkey, but the label doesn't say exactly what they come from: poultry, poultry meal, poultry by-product meal, poultry by-products, poultry fat, hydrolyzed poultry protein, hydrolyzed poultry liver, poultry liver, poultry heart, poultry gizzards, poultry broth, poultry digest, natural poultry flavor, dried poultry, dehydrated poultry protein, poultry giblets, dried poultry plasma, natural flavor, meat, meat and animal derivatives, animal protein, liver, bone broth, broth.

"Poultry" may or may not include turkey, and generic terms like meat, animal protein, liver or broth don't say which animal they came from.

Fats, oils and hydrolyzed forms: less protein, not none

Food allergies are reactions to proteins (Merck Veterinary Manual), and processing changes how much intact protein an ingredient carries. Pawfood rates these as reduced carryover: turkey fat, hydrolyzed turkey protein, natural turkey flavor. Reduced is not zero. Fats and oils can hold traces of protein, and hydrolysis breaks proteins into smaller pieces without removing every reactive fragment. Whether your pet can have these forms is a question for your vet.

How often it's reported in dogs and cats

Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud (2016) pooled published studies of dogs and cats with food allergy. Their most often reported sources for dogs were beef, dairy, chicken, wheat, soy, lamb, corn, egg, pork, fish and rice; for cats, beef, fish and chicken led the list. Turkey is not among the main sources they list. That doesn't rule it out. Food allergies are reactions to proteins a pet has eaten before (Merck Veterinary Manual), so any protein can be involved.

Label checklist for turkey

  • Read the full ingredients list, not the front of the pack. Under AAFCO naming rules a "beef dinner" needs only 25% beef, and "with" or "flavor" names need far less.
  • Check every name in the table above, including fats, broths, digests and plasma.
  • Treat unnamed wordings ("meat", "animal fat", "natural flavor") as unknown and ask the manufacturer what they are made from.
  • Read inside parentheses and the vitamin and mineral part of the list.
  • In a recipe named "turkey", look further down for chicken fat or chicken meal if your pet avoids chicken too.
  • Recheck each new bag and each product separately: recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed.

Treats, toppers and chews

Turkey necks, turkey jerky, holiday-themed treats and broths are common sources. Check treats, chews, toppers and flavored supplements like food, and ask your vet before giving any during an elimination diet.

How Pawfood flags turkey

When turkey is on a pet's avoid list, Pawfood compares each ingredient it recognizes with the lists above:

  • A listed turkey ingredient: Match found if you saved turkey as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you just avoid; Worth a closer look if you saved it as a suspected sensitivity.
  • A reduced-carryover form (fat, oil, hydrolyzed, flavor): still a match for a vet-confirmed allergy, with a note that it carries less protein.
  • A term that might mean turkey: Worth a closer look, because the label doesn't name the source.
  • No recorded match: nothing on the listed ingredients matched your records. It doesn't mean the food is allergen-free; labels can be incomplete and recipes change.

Words Pawfood doesn't recognize are shown as unrecognized, so check those against the label photo yourself.

Talk to your vet

Pawfood explains labels; it doesn't diagnose, treat or prescribe diets. Food allergy is usually confirmed with an elimination diet and food challenge run by a vet, so talk to yours before removing turkey or switching foods. If your pet has trouble breathing, facial swelling, repeated vomiting or collapses, contact a vet or emergency clinic right away.
