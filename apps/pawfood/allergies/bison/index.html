Bison allergy in dogs and cats: what to check on the label

Last updated 7 November 2026 · By Pawfood

If your vet has confirmed a bison allergy in your dog or cat, or you simply avoid bison, the ingredients list is where you check. This page lists the label names that always mean bison, the vaguer terms that might, and how Pawfood flags both.

What counts as bison

Bison covers meat, meal, liver, broth and dried bison from American bison. Pawfood keeps bison separate from beef and from water buffalo, but bison is a close relative of cattle; whether a pet that reacts to beef should also avoid bison is a question for your vet.

Terms that might mean bison

The KB has no vague term linked to bison specifically. Even so, generic wordings such as meat, meat meal or animal fat don't name their source, and "bison" recipes often add other proteins.

Fats, oils and hydrolyzed forms: less protein, not none

Food allergies are reactions to proteins (Merck Veterinary Manual). Every bison ingredient on the KB list is rated high protein carryover, so each one carries bison protein.

How often it's reported in dogs and cats

Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud (2016) pooled published studies of dogs and cats with food allergy. Their most often reported sources for dogs were beef, dairy, chicken, wheat, soy, lamb, corn, egg, pork, fish and rice; for cats, beef, fish and chicken led the list. Bison is not among the main sources they list. That doesn't rule it out. Food allergies are reactions to proteins a pet has eaten before (Merck Veterinary Manual), so any protein can be involved.

Label checklist for bison

  • Read the full ingredients list, not the front of the pack. Under AAFCO naming rules a "beef dinner" needs only 25% beef, and "with" or "flavor" names need far less.
  • Check every name in the table above, including fats, broths, digests and plasma.
  • Treat unnamed wordings ("meat", "animal fat", "natural flavor") as unknown and ask the manufacturer what they are made from.
  • Read inside parentheses and the vitamin and mineral part of the list.
  • Bison-led recipes frequently also list beef, lamb or poultry ingredients. Read the whole list.
  • Recheck each new bag and each product separately: recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed.

Treats, toppers and chews

Bison jerky and bison-flavored chews exist; check whether the flavor comes from bison or from another meat. Check treats, chews, toppers and flavored supplements like food, and ask your vet before giving any during an elimination diet.

How Pawfood flags bison

When bison is on a pet's avoid list, Pawfood compares each ingredient it recognizes with the lists above:

  • A listed bison ingredient: Match found if you saved bison as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you just avoid; Worth a closer look if you saved it as a suspected sensitivity.
  • A reduced-carryover form (fat, oil, hydrolyzed, flavor): still a match for a vet-confirmed allergy, with a note that it carries less protein.
  • A term that might mean bison: Worth a closer look, because the label doesn't name the source.
  • No recorded match: nothing on the listed ingredients matched your records. It doesn't mean the food is allergen-free; labels can be incomplete and recipes change.

Words Pawfood doesn't recognize are shown as unrecognized, so check those against the label photo yourself.

Talk to your vet

Pawfood explains labels; it doesn't diagnose, treat or prescribe diets. Food allergy is usually confirmed with an elimination diet and food challenge run by a vet, so talk to yours before removing bison or switching foods. If your pet has trouble breathing, facial swelling, repeated vomiting or collapses, contact a vet or emergency clinic right away.
