Insect protein allergy in dogs and cats: what to check on the label

Last updated 7 November 2026 · By Pawfood

If your vet has confirmed a insect allergy in your dog or cat, or you simply avoid insect, the ingredients list is where you check. This page lists the label names that always mean insect, the vaguer terms that might, and how Pawfood flags both.

What counts as insect

Insect protein covers black soldier fly larvae (whole, meal and oil), mealworms, crickets and silkworm pupae. Insect-based foods are often sold as novel protein diets; novel only means your pet hasn't eaten it before. Insects are arthropods, like shrimp and krill, so ask your vet whether a shellfish allergy matters for insect foods.

Terms that might mean insect

These wordings can contain insect, but the label doesn't say exactly what they come from: chitosan.

Chitosan can come from insect or crustacean shells or from fungi, and labels rarely say which.

Fats, oils and hydrolyzed forms: less protein, not none

Food allergies are reactions to proteins (Merck Veterinary Manual), and processing changes how much intact protein an ingredient carries. Pawfood rates these as reduced carryover: black soldier fly larvae oil. Reduced is not zero. Fats and oils can hold traces of protein, and hydrolysis breaks proteins into smaller pieces without removing every reactive fragment. Whether your pet can have these forms is a question for your vet.

How often it's reported in dogs and cats

Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud (2016) pooled published studies of dogs and cats with food allergy. Their most often reported sources for dogs were beef, dairy, chicken, wheat, soy, lamb, corn, egg, pork, fish and rice; for cats, beef, fish and chicken led the list. Insect is not among the main sources they list. That doesn't rule it out. Food allergies are reactions to proteins a pet has eaten before (Merck Veterinary Manual), so any protein can be involved.

Label checklist for insect

  • Read the full ingredients list, not the front of the pack. Under AAFCO naming rules a "beef dinner" needs only 25% beef, and "with" or "flavor" names need far less.
  • Check every name in the table above, including fats, broths, digests and plasma.
  • Treat unnamed wordings ("meat", "animal fat", "natural flavor") as unknown and ask the manufacturer what they are made from.
  • Read inside parentheses and the vitamin and mineral part of the list.
  • Insect recipes may also contain fish oil, egg or poultry fat; check each.
  • Look for "Hermetia illucens", the species name of black soldier fly, on EU labels.
  • Recheck each new bag and each product separately: recipes change, and shared equipment isn't listed.

Treats, toppers and chews

Insect-based biscuits and training treats are increasingly common, sometimes mixed with other proteins. Check treats, chews, toppers and flavored supplements like food, and ask your vet before giving any during an elimination diet.

How Pawfood flags insect

When insect is on a pet's avoid list, Pawfood compares each ingredient it recognizes with the lists above:

  • A listed insect ingredient: Match found if you saved insect as a vet-confirmed allergy or as something you just avoid; Worth a closer look if you saved it as a suspected sensitivity.
  • A reduced-carryover form (fat, oil, hydrolyzed, flavor): still a match for a vet-confirmed allergy, with a note that it carries less protein.
  • A term that might mean insect: Worth a closer look, because the label doesn't name the source.
  • No recorded match: nothing on the listed ingredients matched your records. It doesn't mean the food is allergen-free; labels can be incomplete and recipes change.

Words Pawfood doesn't recognize are shown as unrecognized, so check those against the label photo yourself.

Talk to your vet

Pawfood explains labels; it doesn't diagnose, treat or prescribe diets. Food allergy is usually confirmed with an elimination diet and food challenge run by a vet, so talk to yours before removing insect or switching foods. If your pet has trouble breathing, facial swelling, repeated vomiting or collapses, contact a vet or emergency clinic right away.
