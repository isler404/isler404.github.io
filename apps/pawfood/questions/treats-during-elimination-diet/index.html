Can I give treats during an elimination diet?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Only treats your vet approves, usually made from the trial diet itself. Ordinary treats, chews and flavored medicines can undo the trial.

  2. Easy-to-forget items
  3. Treat ideas to discuss with your vet

Only treats your vet approves. During an elimination diet, your pet should eat nothing but the trial food and water, because even small amounts of another protein can keep signs going and make the trial hard to interpret. Many vets suggest using pieces of the trial diet as treats, or a treat made by the same diet's manufacturer for that purpose.

Why small amounts matter

An elimination diet usually lasts at least 8 weeks (Olivry, Mueller and Prélaud 2015). Its purpose is to see whether signs settle when certain proteins are removed. A daily chew or a spoonful of cheese for a pill reintroduces protein and can hide the result. If the signs don't improve, you won't know whether it was the diet or the extras.

Easy-to-forget items

  • dental chews, rawhide, bully sticks and other chews;
  • pill pockets, cheese, peanut butter or meat used to give medicine;
  • flavored medicines, supplements and toothpaste (ask your vet about alternatives);
  • toppers, broths and gravy;
  • food from other pets' bowls, table scraps and things picked up on walks;
  • treats from visitors, neighbors or daycare.

Treat ideas to discuss with your vet

  • Kibble from the trial diet, given from the daily amount.
  • The wet version of the trial diet baked into small pieces, if your vet agrees.
  • Plain foods your vet confirms fit the trial.

Write everything down, including slip-ups. A note like "ate a chicken treat on day 18" helps your vet understand any flare.

After the trial

Once your vet has finished the trial and any food challenges, you can test treats one at a time. More on hidden proteins in treats and toppers.
