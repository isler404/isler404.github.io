What's the difference between 'beef', 'beef dinner', 'with beef' and 'beef flavor'?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

US naming rules tie the name to the amount: 'Beef' needs 95% beef (excluding water), 'Beef Dinner' 25%, 'with Beef' 3%, 'Beef Flavor' only a detectable amount.

In the US, AAFCO naming rules link a product's name to how much of the named ingredient it contains. "Beef Dog Food" must be at least 95% beef, not counting added water; "Beef Dinner" (or entrée, platter, formula) at least 25%; "with Beef" at least 3%; and "Beef Flavor" only enough to be detected. None of these rules says the food contains no other meats.

The four rules

Name on the packMinimum of the named ingredient
"Beef" (95% rule)95% excluding added water; 70% of the total product
"Beef Dinner", "Entrée", "Platter", "Formula" (25% rule)25% excluding added water; 10% of the total product
"with Beef" (3% rule)3%
"Beef Flavor" (flavor rule)Enough to be detectable; can come from beef fat, broth or digest

If two ingredients are named, for example "Beef and Liver Dinner", together they must meet the minimum, and each must be at least 3%.

The trap for allergies

A "Beef Dinner" can be 75% other things, including chicken. A "with Salmon" cat food can be mostly chicken. Front-of-pack names highlight one ingredient; the ingredient list tells you everything that's in it. If your pet has an avoid list, read the whole list every time.

EU labels

EU rules work differently: if a label emphasizes an ingredient in the name or with pictures, it usually states the percentage, for example "with beef (4%)". Category names such as "meat and animal derivatives" may hide the species unless a percentage of a named animal is given.
