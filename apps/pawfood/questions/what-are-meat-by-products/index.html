What are meat by-products in pet food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Meat by-products are clean animal parts other than muscle meat, such as organs, from mammals. The species isn't named, and the US term excludes poultry.

Meat by-products are the clean parts of slaughtered mammals other than muscle meat: organs such as liver, kidneys, lungs and spleen, as well as blood, bone and cleaned stomachs and intestines. Hair, horns, teeth and hooves are excluded. In the US definition, "meat" means mammals, so poultry isn't included, but the label doesn't say whether the source was cattle, pigs, sheep or goats.

By-product isn't a quality grade

"By-product" means something produced alongside a main product, here meat for people. Many by-products, such as liver and heart, are nutrient-dense, and wild carnivores eat them first. Quality depends on freshness, handling and the rest of the formula, not the word itself. More in by-products and meals.

Named vs unnamed

  • "Beef by-products" or "chicken by-products" name the species.
  • "Meat by-products" names only the group (mammals).
  • "Poultry by-products" names birds but not which ones.
  • "Meat by-product meal" is the rendered, dried form.

On EU labels you'll more often see "meat and animal derivatives", a category name that can include muscle, organs and by-products of any farmed land animal, including poultry, unless the label names a species, for example "(of which chicken 4%)". See vague label terms.

Why it matters for allergies

For a pet with no avoid list, the species usually doesn't matter. For a pet with a confirmed beef or pork allergy, "meat by-products" might contain it. Organs carry the same species proteins as muscle meat, so a beef-allergic pet can react to beef liver as well as beef. Ask the manufacturer which species are used and whether that changes between batches.
