Does a chicken allergy mean my dog can't have eggs?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Not necessarily. Egg and chicken meat contain different proteins, and evidence on overlap in dogs and cats is limited. Your vet decides whether eggs fit.

Not necessarily. Egg and chicken meat come from the same bird but contain mostly different proteins: egg white and yolk proteins on one side, muscle proteins on the other. A dog can react to one and not the other. Evidence on overlap in dogs and cats is limited, so ask your vet before giving eggs to a chicken-allergic dog, especially during an elimination diet.

Why they're different

Food allergies are reactions to specific proteins. The main proteins in eggs, such as those in egg white, are not the same as the muscle proteins in chicken meat. That's why many allergy resources, including Pawfood, treat egg as its own family rather than as part of chicken.

How often egg is reported

In a review of published studies of dogs with food allergies, egg was reported in about 4% of dogs, compared with about 15% for chicken (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016). Many dogs reacted to more than one food. These figures describe studies, not your dog's risk.

Where egg appears

Egg is common in pet food and treats, often in forms that are easy to overlook:

  • whole egg, dried egg product, egg powder;
  • egg yolk, egg white, hydrolysed egg protein;
  • eggshell or eggshell membrane (a calcium or joint supplement source; these carry less egg protein, but not necessarily none);
  • treats and biscuits, where egg is used as a binder.

During an elimination diet

Vets usually ask you to avoid every protein your dog has eaten before, unless the trial diet includes it. If your dog has eaten egg in its old food or treats, it may need to be left out for the trial and tested separately afterwards.

How Pawfood shows this

Pawfood keeps egg and chicken as separate families. Saving chicken doesn't flag egg, and saving egg doesn't flag chicken. If your vet asks you to avoid both, save both, and Pawfood checks each label for every egg and chicken form it knows. See the egg and chicken pages.
