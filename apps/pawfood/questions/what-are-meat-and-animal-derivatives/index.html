What does 'meat and animal derivatives' mean on pet food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

It's an EU category name covering meat, organs and by-products of farmed land animals. Unless the label names a species, you can't tell which animals.

"Meat and animal derivatives" is a category name allowed under EU feed labelling rules. It covers the flesh, organs and other parts of farmed land animals, which can include chicken, beef, pork, lamb and others. Unless the label adds a named species, for example "(of which chicken 4%)", you can't tell which animals are in it.

Why category names exist

EU law lets manufacturers list ingredients either individually ("chicken, rice, beet pulp") or by category ("meat and animal derivatives, cereals, derivatives of vegetable origin"). Categories let a recipe change raw materials without reprinting the pack. FEDIAF's labelling code describes how these categories are used and how named ingredients and percentages are declared.

What a percentage tells you

"Meat and animal derivatives (of which beef 4%)" tells you there's at least 4% beef. It doesn't tell you what the other meat and animal derivatives are. They could include chicken or pork.

Similarly, a "with beef" claim on the front relates to the declared percentage, not to the whole meat content.

Other category names

  • Fish and fish derivatives: fish of any species.
  • Derivatives of vegetable origin: plant by-products, such as sugar beet pulp.
  • Cereals: any cereal grain, possibly wheat.
  • Oils and fats: animal or vegetable fats.
  • Milk and milk derivatives, eggs and egg derivatives.

Why it matters for allergies

For a pet with a confirmed beef or chicken allergy, "meat and animal derivatives" is an unknown. During an elimination diet, vets usually ask you to avoid category-labelled foods, because the species can't be checked. Contact the manufacturer and ask which species are included and whether they change between batches.
