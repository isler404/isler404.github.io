Where do I find the calories on a pet food label?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Look for the calorie statement, usually 'kcal ME/kg' plus kcal per cup or can, near the guaranteed analysis. EU labels may show kcal per 100 g.

On US labels, look for the calorie statement near the guaranteed analysis. It is written as kilocalories of metabolizable energy per kilogram ("kcal ME/kg") and usually also per cup, can or piece. On EU labels, energy is often given in kcal or MJ per 100 g or per kg. Treat the feeding guide as a starting point and adjust it with your vet based on your pet's weight and body condition.

Reading the calorie statement

  • kcal/kg lets you compare foods by weight.
  • kcal per cup or can helps with portions, but cups vary in size and in how densely they're filled. Weighing food on a kitchen scale is more consistent.
  • "ME" means metabolizable energy: the energy available to the animal after losses in feces and urine. It's usually calculated from the guaranteed analysis, sometimes measured in feeding tests.

Why calories matter

Two foods that look similar can differ substantially in energy. Fat carries more than twice the energy of protein or carbohydrate per gram, and fiber and moisture dilute energy. Switching to a food with more kcal per cup without changing the scoop size means more calories per day.

Treats count too

Treats, chews and toppers add calories. Their labels often give kcal per piece. WSAVA's nutrition guidelines encourage including treats in the daily calorie picture.

Calculating per day

Daily calories = amount fed × kcal per unit. For example, 2 cups of a food with 380 kcal per cup is 760 kcal. Your pet's needs depend on age, size, activity and neuter status; your vet can estimate a daily target and help you adjust it.

When calories aren't stated

Some labels, especially outside the US and on treats, don't give energy. Don't assume a number from a similar product. The manufacturer can usually tell you.
