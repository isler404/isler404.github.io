How do I know if my cat food has fish in it?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Read the whole ingredient list, not the front of the pack. Fish hides in oils, digests, broths and vague terms like 'ocean fish' or 'animal digest'.

On this page

  2. Where fish appears on cat food labels

Read the full ingredient list on the back of the pack, not the product name. Fish can appear as a named species (salmon, tuna, whitefish), as fish meal, fish oil, fish broth or fish digest, or inside vague terms such as "ocean fish", "animal digest" or "natural flavor". If a term doesn't name its source, ask the manufacturer.

Why the product name isn't enough

A "chicken" cat food can still contain fish. Under AAFCO naming rules, a product called "with chicken" only needs a small amount of chicken, and nothing on the front has to mention the other proteins. Fish oil is often added for omega-3 fatty acids, and fish broth or digest for flavor, even in recipes led by poultry or beef.

Where fish appears on cat food labels

  • Named fish: salmon, tuna, whitefish, cod, sardine, herring, mackerel, pollock, trout and others.
  • Processed forms: fish meal, fish oil, salmon oil, fish broth, fish gelatin, hydrolyzed fish protein, fish digest.
  • Vague terms: "fish", "ocean fish", "seafood", "fish by-products". These tell you it is fish but not which species.
  • Unnamed animal terms: "animal digest", "natural flavor", "meat and animal derivatives" (EU) and "derivatives of vegetable origin" don't name a species at all. Animal digest and natural flavor can be made from fish.
  • Supplements: omega-3 sources such as fish oil, krill oil or "marine oil" in the additives section.

Fish oil is mostly fat and carries less protein than fish meat, but not necessarily none. Whether your cat can have it is a question for your vet.

Why it matters

Fish is one of the most often reported food allergen sources in cats: about 17% of cats in pooled published studies, after beef (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016). Those figures describe published cases, not your cat's odds. More in common cat food allergens.

Remember treats, toppers, dental chews and pill pockets too: they often contain fish.
