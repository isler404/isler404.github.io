What does 'complete and balanced' mean on pet food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

It means the food is designed to be the only diet for a stated life stage: it has all required nutrients (complete) in the right ratios (balanced).

"Complete and balanced" means the food is intended to be fed as the only diet for the life stage on the label. "Complete" means it contains all the nutrients required for that species and life stage; "balanced" means those nutrients are present in the correct ratios. It is a statement about nutrient levels, not about quality, ingredients or allergies.

How a food earns the claim

In the US, the AAFCO statement shows one of two routes:

  • Formulated: "formulated to meet the nutritional levels established by the AAFCO Dog (or Cat) Food Nutrient Profiles". The recipe was designed, and analysed, to meet those levels.
  • Feeding trials: "animal feeding tests using AAFCO procedures substantiate…". The food was fed to animals under a standard protocol.

A third route, for products similar to one that passed a feeding trial, is rarely used. In the EU, the equivalent wording is complete feed, and FEDIAF's Nutritional Guidelines give the nutrient levels most manufacturers follow.

The life stage matters

The statement always names a life stage: growth (puppies and kittens), adult maintenance, gestation/lactation, or all life stages. A food for "all life stages" must meet growth levels. For dogs, check whether the statement includes or excludes "growth of large size dogs (70 lb or more as an adult)". More in the puppy and kitten label guide.

What it doesn't mean

  • It doesn't mean the food suits every individual pet. Health conditions, allergies and age can change what's appropriate.
  • It doesn't rank foods. Two foods can both be complete and balanced with very different ingredients and nutrient levels.
  • It doesn't apply to treats, toppers or foods labelled "for intermittent or supplemental feeding only" (EU: complementary feed). Those aren't meant to be the whole diet.
