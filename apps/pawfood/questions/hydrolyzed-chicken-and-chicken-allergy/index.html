Can a dog with a chicken allergy react to hydrolyzed chicken?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Some can. Hydrolysis makes chicken protein less likely to be recognized, but how far it is broken down matters; studies found reactions to partial hydrolysates.

Yes, some can. Hydrolysis breaks chicken protein into smaller pieces that the immune system is less likely to recognize, but "less likely" isn't "never". How completely the protein is broken down matters: in one trial, 4 of 10 chicken-allergic dogs had a flare of itching on a hydrolysed chicken liver diet, while none did on an extensively hydrolysed poultry feather diet (Bizikova and Olivry 2016).

What hydrolysis does

Food allergies are immune reactions to proteins. For a reaction, antibodies have to recognize a large enough piece of a protein. Hydrolysis uses enzymes to cut proteins into short fragments (peptides). The smaller the fragments, the less likely the immune system is to recognize them.

Not every hydrolysed ingredient is broken down to the same degree, and labels don't state it. "Hydrolyzed chicken protein" or "hydrolyzed chicken liver" on a label tells you the process, not how far it went.

What studies found

  • Clinical trial: in 10 dogs with confirmed chicken allergy, a diet based on hydrolysed chicken liver led to itch flares in 4 dogs; a diet based on extensively hydrolysed poultry feathers led to none (Bizikova and Olivry 2016).
  • Lab study: blood from dogs with chicken-specific antibodies recognized mildly hydrolysed poultry feathers in 37% of samples and extensively hydrolysed feathers in none (Olivry, Bexley and Mougeot 2017).

These are small studies. They show why vets choose hydrolysed diets carefully, not which product will work for your dog.

Hydrolysed ingredients in ordinary foods

Hydrolysed chicken protein or liver also appears in regular foods and treats as a flavor enhancer. These are not the same as a veterinary hydrolysed diet, and the degree of hydrolysis isn't stated. Treat them as chicken unless your vet says otherwise.

Talk to your vet

If your vet suggests a hydrolysed diet for a diet trial, ask what it is made from, how extensively it is hydrolysed and whether treats and flavored medications fit the plan. More in the hydrolyzed protein diets guide.
