Does 'natural flavor' in pet food contain chicken?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

It can. 'Natural flavor' doesn't name its source, and in pet food it is often made from animal tissue such as chicken. Ask the manufacturer.

It can. "Natural flavor" on a pet food label doesn't say what it is made from. In pet food, flavors are often made by breaking down animal tissue, and chicken is widely used, but the source could also be another animal or a plant. The only way to know is to ask the manufacturer about that specific product.

What "natural flavor" means

"Natural" in pet food means the ingredient comes from plant, animal or mined sources and hasn't been made by chemical synthesis. It doesn't name the species. A named flavor, such as "natural chicken flavor", tells you the main source; an unnamed one doesn't.

Flavors are used in small amounts, usually near the end of the list. Small doesn't mean irrelevant for a pet with a confirmed allergy, especially during an elimination diet.

Related terms to watch

  • Animal digest: animal tissue broken down by enzymes or heat; species often not stated. See vague label terms.
  • Chicken digest, liver digest: named or partially named versions.
  • Natural poultry flavor: could be chicken, turkey, duck or other birds.
  • Hydrolyzed animal protein: flavor enhancer without a named species.

How to get an answer

Contact the manufacturer and ask:

  1. What is the "natural flavor" in this product made from, by species?
  2. Does that apply to every batch, or can the source change?
  3. Is the product made on lines shared with chicken products?

Note the date and the answer. Recipes change, so ask again if the packaging changes.

When it matters most

During an elimination diet, vets usually ask you to avoid any ingredient whose source is unknown, so a food or treat with "natural flavor" may not fit the trial. For a pet with a confirmed chicken allergy, your vet can tell you how cautious to be.
