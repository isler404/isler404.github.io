What's the difference between chicken and chicken meal in pet food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Chicken is fresh or frozen meat that is mostly water. Chicken meal is the same tissue rendered and dried, so it's a concentrated protein source.

"Chicken" on a label means clean chicken flesh and skin, with or without bone, added fresh or frozen, so it is mostly water. "Chicken meal" is the same kind of tissue rendered: cooked, with most of the water and fat removed, and ground. Gram for gram, chicken meal carries far more protein. For allergies, both are chicken.

Why the order on the list can mislead

Ingredients are listed by weight as they go into the recipe, before cooking. Fresh chicken is heavy because of its water, so it can be listed first even if, after the food is cooked and dried, it contributes less protein than a meal listed lower down. A label that starts with "chicken, chicken meal" is getting protein from both.

This isn't a trick; it is how ingredient lists work. It does mean the first ingredient alone doesn't tell you where most of the protein comes from. See how to read pet food labels.

Is meal lower quality?

Not by definition. Rendering is a standard process, and a named meal ("chicken meal") tells you exactly which species it is. Quality depends on the raw material and processing, which the label doesn't show. "Chicken by-product meal" is different: it can include parts such as necks, feet and organs. More in by-products and meals.

Other chicken forms

  • Dried or dehydrated chicken: chicken with water removed, similar in concentration to meal.
  • Chicken fat: mostly fat, with less protein.
  • Chicken broth, chicken digest, hydrolyzed chicken protein: flavor or protein ingredients, still chicken.
  • Poultry meal: could be chicken or other birds.

For pets with a chicken allergy

Every form above counts as chicken, including meal, broth and digest. Fat and hydrolysed forms carry less of the protein, but not none; ask your vet whether they fit your pet's plan. The chicken allergen page lists every label name.
