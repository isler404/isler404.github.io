What is a novel protein diet?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

A novel protein diet uses a protein your pet has never eaten before, such as rabbit, venison or kangaroo, usually for a vet-led elimination trial.

A novel protein diet is built around a protein your pet has never eaten before, so its immune system has had no chance to become sensitized to it. "Novel" is relative to your pet's history, not a type of meat: rabbit is novel for a dog that has only eaten chicken and beef, but not for one that has eaten rabbit treats. These diets are mainly used in vet-led elimination trials.

How it's used

Food allergy in dogs and cats is usually confirmed with an elimination diet followed by a food challenge. For several weeks your pet eats only the trial diet (a novel protein or a hydrolysed diet) plus water. If the signs settle, the old food is reintroduced to see whether they return. Your vet picks the diet based on everything your pet has eaten before.

Choosing a novel protein

Common options include rabbit, venison, kangaroo, horse, duck, goat or insect protein, paired with a carbohydrate your pet hasn't had much of. What counts as novel depends on:

  • Every food, treat and chew your pet has eaten. Write down brands and flavors, including dental chews and pill pockets.
  • Related species. Some animals share similar proteins. Chicken, turkey and duck are related, and dogs with antibodies to chicken often also recognized turkey and duck in lab tests (Olivry, Bexley and Mougeot 2017). Your vet may prefer an unrelated species.
  • Availability. A protein that becomes common in pet food stops being novel for many pets.

Over-the-counter vs veterinary diets

Studies have found that many commercial pet foods contain ingredients not on the label, including foods marketed as "novel" or "limited ingredient" (Olivry and Mueller 2018). That's why vets often prefer veterinary diets or home-prepared recipes for a trial. Ask your vet which they recommend.

Novel protein vs hydrolysed

A hydrolysed diet breaks a familiar protein into small pieces; a novel protein diet avoids familiar proteins altogether. Both are used in elimination trials. See hydrolyzed protein diets.
