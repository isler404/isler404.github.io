What are mixed tocopherols in pet food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Mixed tocopherols are forms of vitamin E used as a natural preservative to slow fats going rancid. They usually come from vegetable oils, often soy.

Mixed tocopherols are a blend of vitamin E compounds (alpha, beta, gamma and delta tocopherol) used as a natural antioxidant preservative. They slow the oxidation of fats, which is what makes a food go rancid. You'll often see them in brackets after a fat, as in "chicken fat (preserved with mixed tocopherols)". They are usually extracted from vegetable oils, most often soybean oil.

Preservative, not vitamin

As a preservative, mixed tocopherols protect the fats in the food. They are counted differently from the vitamin E added as a nutrient, which usually appears as "vitamin E supplement" in the vitamin section. Some vitamin E activity remains, but the preservative role is the reason they're there.

Natural vs synthetic antioxidants

  • Natural: mixed tocopherols, rosemary extract, citric acid, ascorbyl palmitate (vitamin C).
  • Synthetic: BHA, BHT, TBHQ, propyl gallate and, in some regions, ethoxyquin.

Natural antioxidants are generally less potent and break down faster, which is why they're often combined (for example, tocopherols with rosemary extract and citric acid) and why opened bags should be stored cool, dry and closed. More in preservatives explained.

Soy and allergies

Mixed tocopherols are often derived from soybean oil, but they are highly refined and carry very little soy protein. Food allergies are reactions to proteins. If your vet has asked you to avoid soy strictly, ask the manufacturer about the source and whether it matters for your pet.

What to check

  • Look for preservatives in brackets after fats and oils, not only in the main list.
  • Some foods name the tocopherol source; most don't.
  • Freshness depends on storage too. Check the best-before date and keep the bag closed.
