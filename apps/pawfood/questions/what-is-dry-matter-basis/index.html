What is dry matter basis and how do I calculate it?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Dry matter basis means a nutrient value with the water removed. Divide the label value by (100 − moisture) and multiply by 100.

Dry matter basis is a nutrient value recalculated as if all the water were removed from the food. To calculate it, subtract the moisture percentage from 100 to get the dry matter, then divide the label value by the dry matter and multiply by 100. It lets you compare wet and dry foods fairly.

Why you need it

The guaranteed analysis on a pet food label is given as fed, water included. A can of wet food is often around three-quarters water, while kibble is around one-tenth. So a wet food's protein percentage looks much lower than a kibble's even when, once the water is gone, it has as much or more.

The two formulas

Dry matter % = 100 − moisture %

Dry matter value = as-fed value ÷ dry matter % × 100

A worked example

Wet foodDry food
Crude protein (min.)10%30%
Moisture (max.)78%10%
Dry matter22%90%
Protein, dry matter basis10 ÷ 22 × 100 ≈ 45%30 ÷ 90 × 100 ≈ 33%

On a dry matter basis, this wet food has more protein than the kibble, not less. The same conversion works for fat, fiber and ash. The numbers in this example are illustrations, not typical values for any product.

Things to keep in mind

  • Minimums and maximums. Protein and fat on the label are minimums, moisture and fiber are maximums, so the result is an estimate.
  • Crude protein isn't meat. Plant proteins such as pea protein or corn gluten meal raise crude protein too.
  • Missing isn't zero. If the label doesn't state a value, you can't convert it, and a carbohydrate estimate needs moisture, protein, fat, fiber and ash.
  • Energy matters too. Nutritionists often compare nutrients per 1,000 kcal, because foods differ in calories as well as water.
