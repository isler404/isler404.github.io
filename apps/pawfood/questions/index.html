Questions and answers

Short, direct answers about dog and cat food labels. For anything about your own pet's health, ask your veterinarian.

  • Can a blood, hair or saliva test diagnose food allergies in dogs and cats?Not reliably. Blood tests for food allergy proved inaccurate in reviews, and hair and saliva tests are unproven. A vet-led elimination diet is the standard.
  • Can a dog with a chicken allergy react to hydrolyzed chicken?Some can. Hydrolysis makes chicken protein less likely to be recognized, but how far it is broken down matters; studies found reactions to partial hydrolysates.
  • Can I give treats during an elimination diet?Only treats your vet approves, usually made from the trial diet itself. Ordinary treats, chews and flavored medicines can undo the trial.
  • Can pet food contain ingredients that aren't on the label?Yes. Studies testing pet foods found undeclared animal or plant ingredients in many products, including some sold for elimination diets.
  • Does 'natural flavor' in pet food contain chicken?It can. 'Natural flavor' doesn't name its source, and in pet food it is often made from animal tissue such as chicken. Ask the manufacturer.
  • Does a chicken allergy mean my dog can't have eggs?Not necessarily. Egg and chicken meat contain different proteins, and evidence on overlap in dogs and cats is limited. Your vet decides whether eggs fit.
  • Does the first ingredient tell me what pet food is mostly made of?Only partly. Ingredients are listed by weight before cooking, so water-heavy fresh meat can come first even if dry ingredients supply more nutrients.
  • How do I calculate the carbohydrates in pet food?Subtract protein, fat, fiber, moisture and ash from 100. It only works when all five are on the label, and the result is an estimate.
  • How do I know if my cat food has fish in it?Read the whole ingredient list, not the front of the pack. Fish hides in oils, digests, broths and vague terms like 'ocean fish' or 'animal digest'.
  • How do I read a cat food label?Check the adequacy statement first, then the ingredient list, the guaranteed analysis and the calories. The product name is the least reliable part.
  • How do I switch my dog to a new food?Mix the new food into the old one gradually over several days, increasing the new share step by step, and note how your dog's stool and appetite respond.
  • How long should a food elimination trial last?A review found that at least 8 weeks are needed to catch more than 90% of food-allergic dogs and cats. Your vet sets the length for your pet.
  • If my dog is allergic to chicken, can it have turkey or duck?Maybe not. Birds share similar proteins, and lab studies found most chicken-sensitized dogs also recognized turkey and duck. Ask your vet before trying.
  • Is brewers rice bad for dogs and cats?No. Brewers rice is small broken pieces of rice kernels left from milling, a digestible carbohydrate source. It's a by-product, not a quality warning.
  • Is chicken fat okay for a dog with a chicken allergy?Chicken fat carries far less protein than chicken meat, but not none. Whether a chicken-allergic dog can have it is a decision for your vet.
  • Is grain-free food better for dogs with allergies?Not automatically. Most reported dog food allergies involve animal proteins, not grains. Grain-free only helps if a grain is what your dog reacts to.
  • Is there an app to scan dog food ingredients for allergies?Yes. Pawfood is an iPhone app that scans dog and cat food labels and checks each ingredient against your pet's allergens and avoid list.
  • What are BHA, BHT and ethoxyquin in pet food?Synthetic antioxidants that keep fats from going rancid. BHA and BHT are permitted within limits; the EU no longer authorizes ethoxyquin in feed.
  • What are meat by-products in pet food?Meat by-products are clean animal parts other than muscle meat, such as organs, from mammals. The species isn't named, and the US term excludes poultry.
  • What are mixed tocopherols in pet food?Mixed tocopherols are forms of vitamin E used as a natural preservative to slow fats going rancid. They usually come from vegetable oils, often soy.
  • What are the most common cat food allergens?In published studies, beef, fish and chicken were the most often reported food allergen sources in cats, followed by wheat. Not a risk ranking for your cat.
  • What are the most common dog food allergens?In published studies, beef, dairy, chicken and wheat were the most often reported food allergen sources in dogs. That isn't a ranking of risk for your dog.
  • What does 'complete and balanced' mean on pet food?It means the food is designed to be the only diet for a stated life stage: it has all required nutrients (complete) in the right ratios (balanced).
  • What does 'crude protein' mean on dog food?Crude protein is total protein estimated from nitrogen content. 'Crude' refers to the lab method, not quality, and it includes plant and animal protein.
  • What does 'for intermittent or supplemental feeding only' mean?The product isn't nutritionally complete, so it isn't meant as the main diet. Seen on treats, toppers and some wet foods; EU: 'complementary feed'.
  • What does 'limited ingredient' mean on pet food?It's a marketing term for a recipe with fewer ingredients, often one main protein. It has no set legal definition and isn't a medical claim.
  • What does 'meat and animal derivatives' mean on pet food?It's an EU category name covering meat, organs and by-products of farmed land animals. Unless the label names a species, you can't tell which animals.
  • What does 'poultry meal' mean on dog food?Poultry meal is rendered, dried poultry tissue. 'Poultry' doesn't name the bird, so it may be chicken, turkey, duck or a mix.
  • What does the guaranteed analysis on pet food mean?It lists minimum crude protein and fat and maximum fiber and moisture, as fed. They're limits, not exact amounts, and need converting to compare foods.
  • What is a novel protein diet?A novel protein diet uses a protein your pet has never eaten before, such as rabbit, venison or kangaroo, usually for a vet-led elimination trial.
  • What is animal digest in pet food?Animal digest is animal tissue broken down by enzymes or heat into a liquid or powder flavor coating. The species is often not stated.
  • What is dry matter basis and how do I calculate it?Dry matter basis means a nutrient value with the water removed. Divide the label value by (100 − moisture) and multiply by 100.
  • What is pea protein and why is it in dog food?Pea protein is concentrated protein from peas. It raises the food's protein level at lower cost. It counts as plant protein, not meat.
  • What is taurine and why do cats need it?Taurine is an amino acid cats can't make in sufficient amounts, so it must come from food. Low taurine has been linked to heart and eye disease in cats.
  • What signs are reported in dogs and cats with food allergies?Mostly itching (ears, paws, belly or face), recurring skin and ear infections, and sometimes vomiting or diarrhea. Your vet works out the reason.
  • What's the difference between 'beef', 'beef dinner', 'with beef' and 'beef flavor'?US naming rules tie the name to the amount: 'Beef' needs 95% beef (excluding water), 'Beef Dinner' 25%, 'with Beef' 3%, 'Beef Flavor' only a detectable amount.
  • What's the difference between chicken and chicken meal in pet food?Chicken is fresh or frozen meat that is mostly water. Chicken meal is the same tissue rendered and dried, so it's a concentrated protein source.
  • Where do I find the calories on a pet food label?Look for the calorie statement, usually 'kcal ME/kg' plus kcal per cup or can, near the guaranteed analysis. EU labels may show kcal per 100 g.
  • Why does wet food show less protein than dry food?Because wet food is mostly water and label percentages include the water. Remove it (dry matter basis) and wet food often has as much protein or more.
  • Why isn't propylene glycol allowed in cat food?A US FDA regulation bars propylene glycol in or on cat food. In cats it has been associated with red blood cell changes. It's still used in some dog treats.
