What does 'poultry meal' mean on dog food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Poultry meal is rendered, dried poultry tissue. 'Poultry' doesn't name the bird, so it may be chicken, turkey, duck or a mix.

  3. Why it matters for allergies

Poultry meal is poultry tissue that has been cooked, had most of its water and fat removed, and been ground into a dry protein ingredient. "Poultry" means domestic birds in general, so the label doesn't tell you whether it is chicken, turkey, duck or a mix. For most dogs that makes no difference; for a dog with a chicken or turkey allergy, it is an open question.

What "meal" means

"Meal" is a feed term for a rendered ingredient. Rendering cooks the raw material, separates the fat and dries what is left. The result is concentrated: a meal holds far more protein per gram than fresh meat, which is mostly water. That is why a meal listed third can still supply a lot of the protein in a kibble. Being a meal says nothing bad about quality; see by-products and meals for the definitions.

What "poultry" means

"Poultry" is a collective name. Feed rules allow it so manufacturers can buy birds on the open market without reprinting the bag each time the species mix changes. Chicken is the most common poultry in pet food, but the word doesn't promise chicken, and it doesn't rule out turkey, duck or others.

Poultry meal is different from poultry by-product meal. By-product meal may include parts such as necks, feet and organs, while poultry meal is made mainly from flesh and skin, with or without bone. Both leave the species open.

Why it matters for allergies

If your vet has told you to avoid one bird, a named meal ("chicken meal", "turkey meal") gives you a clear answer. "Poultry meal" doesn't. Research on dogs and cats with chicken-specific antibodies found that most of their blood samples also recognized turkey and duck meat (Olivry, Bexley and Mougeot 2017), so your vet may ask you to avoid poultry as a group. Antibody recognition in a lab isn't the same as a reaction in your pet, which is why this is a decision for your vet.

To find out which birds are in a specific product, ask the manufacturer for the species in that recipe, and ask again if the recipe changes.
