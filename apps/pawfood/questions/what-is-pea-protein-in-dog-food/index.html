What is pea protein and why is it in dog food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Pea protein is concentrated protein from peas. It raises the food's protein level at lower cost. It counts as plant protein, not meat.

Pea protein is the protein fraction of peas, separated from the starch and fiber and concentrated. Manufacturers add it to raise a food's protein level, often in grain-free recipes. On the label it counts toward crude protein like meat does, but it is plant protein with a different amino acid profile.

Why it's used

  • Protein and cost. It's a concentrated, relatively affordable protein source.
  • Grain-free recipes. Peas, pea starch and pea fiber often replace grains; pea protein fills the protein gap.
  • Texture. It helps form and bind kibble.

What to watch on the label

Crude protein isn't meat content. The guaranteed analysis doesn't split plant and animal protein. A high protein number may partly come from peas, potato protein or corn gluten meal.

Ingredient splitting. "Peas, pea protein, pea starch, pea fiber" can each appear lower on the list than a single combined entry would. Mentally group them when you judge what the food is built on. See how to read pet food labels.

Pea protein and DCM

Between 2018 and 2022 the U.S. FDA investigated reports of dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) in dogs eating certain diets, many labelled grain-free with peas, lentils or potatoes high on the list. The FDA said its report numbers alone could not establish a link to specific products, and research continues. If your dog eats a pea-rich diet, discuss it with your vet. More in grain-free explained.

Allergies

Peas were not among the most often reported food allergen sources in published dog and cat studies (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016), but any protein can be involved in an individual pet. Peas belong to the legume family, alongside lentils, chickpeas, beans and soy.
