What are the most common dog food allergens?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

In published studies, beef, dairy, chicken and wheat were the most often reported food allergen sources in dogs. That isn't a ranking of risk for your dog.

In a review of published studies covering 297 dogs with food allergies, the most often reported sources were beef (34%), dairy (17%), chicken (15%) and wheat (13%), followed by soy, lamb, corn and egg (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016). Many dogs reacted to more than one food. These figures reflect which proteins were common in dog diets and in the studies, not your own dog's odds.

How to read this

  • Exposure matters. Beef, dairy and chicken have long been common in dog food. A protein a dog has eaten for years is more likely to show up in the statistics than one it has never eaten.
  • Grains are not the main story. Wheat appears, but animal proteins top the list. A grain-free food isn't automatically a fit for a dog with a food allergy; see grain-free explained.
  • Allergy is a protein reaction. Dogs react to proteins in a food, not to "meat" or "grains" as categories.

How food allergy is confirmed

Blood, hair and saliva tests are not reliable for food allergy in dogs (Mueller and Olivry 2017). Vets confirm it with an elimination diet followed by a food challenge. If you suspect a food allergy, talk to your vet before changing foods. More in food allergy or intolerance.

Hidden sources

Beef, dairy and chicken often appear where you might not look: broths, fats, digests, cheese in treats, "natural flavor" and "animal digest". See the allergen pages for beef, dairy, chicken and wheat.
