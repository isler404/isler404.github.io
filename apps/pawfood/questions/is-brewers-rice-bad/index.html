Is brewers rice bad for dogs and cats?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

No. Brewers rice is small broken pieces of rice kernels left from milling, a digestible carbohydrate source. It's a by-product, not a quality warning.

Not in itself. Brewers rice is small fragments of rice kernels that break off during milling and are separated from the whole grains. Nutritionally it is white rice in smaller pieces: mostly starch, with some protein, and easy to digest once cooked. The name refers to its traditional use in brewing, not to alcohol in the food.

Why it has a bad reputation

Brewers rice is a by-product of producing whole white rice, and "by-product" sounds like waste. In feed language, a by-product is simply something produced alongside a main product. Broken rice has much the same composition as whole white rice; it is cheaper because it is less attractive for people to buy, not because it is a different food.

What it isn't is whole-grain: like white rice, it has lost most of the bran and germ, so it carries less fiber than brown rice. Whether that matters depends on the rest of the recipe.

Does it matter for allergies?

Rice was rarely reported as a food allergen source in dogs and cats in published studies, compared with beef, dairy, chicken and wheat (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016). If your vet has asked you to avoid rice, brewers rice counts as rice, as do rice flour, rice bran and rice protein.

Reading it on a label

  • Position on the list tells you its share by weight before cooking, not its quality.
  • Several rice forms (brewers rice, rice flour, brown rice) may each appear further down the list than they would together.
  • A food's quality depends on the whole formula and whether it is complete and balanced for your pet's life stage, not on one ingredient.
