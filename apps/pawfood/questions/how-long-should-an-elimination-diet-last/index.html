How long should a food elimination trial last?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

A review found that at least 8 weeks are needed to catch more than 90% of food-allergic dogs and cats. Your vet sets the length for your pet.

On this page

  2. What "elimination" means in practice
  3. After the trial
  4. Keep notes
  5. How Pawfood shows this
  6. Sources

Usually at least 8 weeks. A review of published studies found that more than 80% of dogs and cats with food-related skin disease had improved by 5 weeks (dogs) and 6 weeks (cats), and more than 90% by 8 weeks, so the authors recommended trials of at least 8 weeks (Olivry, Mueller and Prélaud 2015). Your vet decides the length for your pet, and may adjust it.

Why it takes so long

Skin turns over slowly, and secondary problems such as skin or ear infections need time to settle. Stopping after two or three weeks can make a food look irrelevant when it isn't. Digestive signs may change sooner, but your vet will tell you what to watch.

What "elimination" means in practice

For the whole trial, your pet eats only the diet your vet chose plus water. That includes:

  • no other treats, chews, toppers or table food;
  • no flavored medications, supplements or toothpaste unless your vet approves them;
  • no access to other pets' bowls or food dropped elsewhere.

One flavored chew can undo weeks of work. See treats and toppers for the items people most often forget.

After the trial

If the signs improve, your vet will usually suggest a food challenge: reintroducing the previous food or single ingredients to see whether the signs come back. In a review of published cases, half of food-allergic dogs flared within 5 days of a challenge and 90% within 14 days; for cats the figures were 4 and 7 days (Olivry and Mueller 2020). The challenge is what confirms the food link, so don't skip it without discussing it with your vet.

Keep notes

A daily journal makes the trial easier to judge: what your pet ate, any slip-ups, itching, stool, vomiting and weight. Bring it to the recheck. The food journal guide lists what to record.
