Why does wet food show less protein than dry food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Because wet food is mostly water and label percentages include the water. Remove it (dry matter basis) and wet food often has as much protein or more.

On this page

Because label percentages include water. Wet food is often around three-quarters water, kibble around one-tenth, so the same amount of protein makes up a much smaller share of the wet food's weight. Once you remove the water and compare on a dry matter basis, wet food often has as much protein as dry food, or more.

A quick example

Wet foodDry food
Crude protein (as fed)9%27%
Moisture78%10%
Dry matter22%90%
Protein, dry matter9 ÷ 22 × 100 ≈ 41%27 ÷ 90 × 100 = 30%

Illustrative numbers, not values for any product. The wet food here has more protein relative to everything else in the food.

How to compare

  1. Find the moisture value in the guaranteed analysis.
  2. Calculate dry matter: 100 − moisture.
  3. Divide each nutrient by the dry matter and multiply by 100.

The dry matter guide explains each step and its limits, and the dry matter calculator does the arithmetic.

Compare calories too

Dry matter removes water, but foods also differ in fat, and fat carries more than twice the energy of protein per gram. Comparing nutrients per 1,000 kcal shows how much your pet actually eats to meet its energy needs. When the label gives kcal/kg, grams per 1,000 kcal = as-fed % × 10,000 ÷ kcal/kg.

What it doesn't mean

A higher dry matter protein isn't automatically better. Needs depend on species, life stage and health, and a wet food isn't more or less complete than a dry one; check the adequacy statement on each. If you mix wet and dry, both count toward the day's calories. More in wet vs dry food labels.

How Pawfood shows this
