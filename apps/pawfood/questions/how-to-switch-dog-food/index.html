How do I switch my dog to a new food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Mix the new food into the old one gradually over several days, increasing the new share step by step, and note how your dog's stool and appetite respond.

Change food gradually rather than overnight. Mix a small share of the new food into the old food, then increase the new share in steps over several days until it replaces the old food completely. Watch your dog's stool, appetite and energy along the way, and slow down if digestion is unsettled. If your dog has a health condition or your vet chose the new food, follow your vet's plan.

A simple step plan

A common pattern uses four steps:

StepNew foodOld food
125%75%
250%50%
375%25%
4100%0%

Spread the steps evenly over the transition. Dogs with sensitive digestion may need longer steps. Many pet food labels give their own transition advice too.

Before you start

  • Read the new label. Check the adequacy statement fits your dog's life stage, and that the ingredient list doesn't contain anything on your dog's avoid list. "Salmon" recipes can contain chicken fat, for example.
  • Compare calories. Foods differ in energy per cup or per gram, so the same scoop can mean more or fewer calories. Use the new feeding guide as a starting point.
  • Keep the old bag. Note the brand, recipe and lot number in case you need to go back or tell your vet.

During the switch

Note each day: the mix you fed, stool consistency, appetite, vomiting and anything unusual. Changes to stool are common during a transition and usually settle. Contact your vet if your dog stops eating, vomits repeatedly, has bloody stool or seems unwell.

When not to mix

During a vet-led elimination diet, your vet may ask you to switch differently, because old food in the bowl affects the trial. Ask before starting.
