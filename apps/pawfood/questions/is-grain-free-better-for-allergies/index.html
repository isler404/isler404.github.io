Is grain-free food better for dogs with allergies?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Not automatically. Most reported dog food allergies involve animal proteins, not grains. Grain-free only helps if a grain is what your dog reacts to.

Not automatically. In published studies, animal proteins such as beef, dairy and chicken were reported far more often than grains in dogs with food allergies, though wheat does appear on the list (Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016). A grain-free food only addresses an allergy if a grain is what your dog reacts to, and it can still contain the animal protein that matters.

What "grain-free" means

Grain-free means the food contains no cereal grains such as wheat, corn, rice, barley or oats. It says nothing about the protein source, and the grains are usually replaced with other carbohydrate sources: peas, lentils, chickpeas, potatoes, sweet potatoes or tapioca. It isn't a low-carbohydrate claim or an allergy claim.

FoodShare of dogs with food allergy in studies
Beefabout 34%

Source: Mueller, Olivry and Prélaud 2016.

A grain-free chicken recipe still contains chicken. For a dog that reacts to chicken, the grain-free label doesn't help.

The FDA and DCM

Between 2018 and 2022 the U.S. FDA investigated reports of dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) in dogs eating certain diets, many labelled grain-free and high in peas, lentils or potatoes. The FDA said its report numbers alone could not establish a link to specific products, and that it would publish further updates only when there is meaningful new scientific information. If you're considering a grain-free diet, discuss it with your vet. Details in grain-free explained.

What actually helps

If you suspect a food allergy, a vet-led elimination diet is the standard way to find out which food is involved. It uses a protein your dog hasn't eaten before, or a hydrolysed diet, for a set period, and grains may or may not be part of it.
