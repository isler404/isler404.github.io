How do I read a cat food label?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Check the adequacy statement first, then the ingredient list, the guaranteed analysis and the calories. The product name is the least reliable part.

Start with the nutritional adequacy statement, which tells you whether the food is a complete diet and for which life stage. Then read the whole ingredient list, the guaranteed analysis and the calorie statement. The product name and front-of-pack claims are the least precise part of the label.

1. Is it a complete food?

Look for a sentence such as "complete and balanced for adult maintenance" (US) or "complete feed" (EU). A food marked "for intermittent or supplemental feeding only" or "complementary feed" isn't meant to be the whole diet. Cats have specific needs, including taurine, so check that the statement names cats and fits your cat's life stage (growth, adult, all life stages).

2. Read the name literally

Under AAFCO naming rules, "Chicken Cat Food" must be mostly chicken, "Chicken Dinner" needs much less, "with chicken" less still, and "chicken flavor" only enough to be detectable. A "salmon" recipe can also contain chicken, beef or fish from other species.

3. Read every ingredient

Ingredients are listed in descending order by weight before cooking. Fresh meats are heavy with water, so they can sit at the top even when dry ingredients supply more nutrients. Look for:

  • the animal proteins, including fats, broths and digests;
  • vague terms that don't name a species ("meat and animal derivatives", "animal digest", "natural flavor");
  • additives, including taurine in the vitamin and mineral section.

4. Read the guaranteed analysis

US labels give minimum crude protein and fat and maximum fiber and moisture. EU labels list "analytical constituents". Wet cat food is mostly water, so compare foods on a dry matter basis.

5. Calories and feeding guide

The calorie statement (kcal per kg, can or cup) and the feeding guide help you portion the food. Feeding guides are starting points; your cat's weight and body condition matter more.

6. The small print

Best-before date, lot number and the manufacturer's contact details. Keep a photo of the label: you'll need the lot number if there's ever a recall question, and contact details to ask about vague ingredients.
