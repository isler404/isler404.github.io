What signs are reported in dogs and cats with food allergies?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Mostly itching (ears, paws, belly or face), recurring skin and ear infections, and sometimes vomiting or diarrhea. Your vet works out the reason.

The most commonly reported sign is itching. In dogs it often affects the ears, paws, belly and face, and shows up as recurring skin or ear infections; in cats it often affects the head, face and neck, with hair loss from overgrooming or scabby skin. Vomiting, diarrhea or more frequent bowel movements are also reported. The same signs occur with other conditions, such as flea or environmental allergies, so only your vet can work out what's behind them.

Skin signs

A review of published cases (Olivry and Mueller 2019) described:

  • Dogs: itching, often of the ears, feet and belly; recurrent bacterial skin infections, ear inflammation (otitis externa) and signs similar to atopic dermatitis. About half had itching all over the body.
  • Cats: most were itchy, especially on the head, face and neck. Common patterns were symmetrical hair loss from overgrooming, scabby lesions of the head and neck, small crusty bumps (miliary dermatitis) and eosinophilic skin lesions. Cats tended to show signs later in life than dogs.

Food allergy can appear at any age and in any breed.

Digestive and other signs

A second review (Mueller and Olivry 2018) found that diarrhea and frequent defecation were the digestive signs most often improved by diet change in dogs, and vomiting and diarrhea in cats. Less often, sneezing, eye inflammation and, rarely, severe allergic reactions were reported.

When to contact a vet right away

Contact a vet or emergency clinic immediately if your pet has trouble breathing, facial swelling, repeated vomiting, bloody diarrhea or collapses. Don't wait to see a pattern.

How a vet finds out

Signs alone can't confirm a food allergy. Vets usually rule out parasites and infections first, then run an elimination diet for at least 8 weeks in most cases, followed by a food challenge. Blood, hair and saliva tests aren't reliable for this. See allergy vs intolerance.

What you can do

Keep notes before your appointment: what your pet eats (including treats), when signs appear, how itchy your pet seems, stool quality and any changes. Photos help. The food journal guide explains what to log.
