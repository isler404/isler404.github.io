Why isn't propylene glycol allowed in cat food?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

A US FDA regulation bars propylene glycol in or on cat food. In cats it has been associated with red blood cell changes. It's still used in some dog treats.

In the US, a specific FDA regulation (21 CFR 589.1001) states that the use of propylene glycol in or on cat food hasn't been supported by adequate scientific data, so cat food containing it is considered adulterated. In cats, propylene glycol has been associated with changes in red blood cells called Heinz bodies. It is still used as a moisture-keeping ingredient in some semi-moist dog foods and treats.

What propylene glycol does

Propylene glycol is a humectant: it holds water, keeping semi-moist foods and treats soft and chewy and helping them resist spoilage. Glycerin and sorbitol do a similar job.

Why cats are different

The Merck Veterinary Manual describes Heinz body formation in cats exposed to propylene glycol, a change in red blood cells that can shorten their lifespan. Cats are more sensitive to this kind of red blood cell damage than many other species. The US rule reflects that difference.

Where you might see it

  • Semi-moist dog foods and soft dog treats.
  • Some flavored products made for dogs.
  • Not in US cat food. If a cat eats dog treats regularly, it may be exposed.

What to do

  • Read treat labels if you share treats between pets.
  • If your cat has eaten a product with propylene glycol, or seems unwell, contact your vet.
  • Regulations outside the US differ, so the ingredient list is what to check on imported products.

How Pawfood shows this

When you scan a product for a cat, Pawfood adds a species note if propylene glycol is on the label. For dogs it is listed and explained like any other ingredient. If you prefer to avoid it for any pet, add it to that pet's list and future scans will highlight it.
