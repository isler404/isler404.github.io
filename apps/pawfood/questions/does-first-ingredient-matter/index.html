Does the first ingredient tell me what pet food is mostly made of?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Only partly. Ingredients are listed by weight before cooking, so water-heavy fresh meat can come first even if dry ingredients supply more nutrients.

Only partly. Ingredients are listed in descending order by weight, but the weight is measured as they go into the recipe, water included. Fresh meat is mostly water, so it can be listed first even when, after cooking and drying, a meal or a grain further down supplies more of the finished food. Read the first several ingredients together, not just the first one.

How the order works

AAFCO rules require ingredients in descending order by weight. The list doesn't give amounts, and the weights are of the raw ingredients. In dry food, most of the water from fresh meat is driven off during cooking, so its contribution to the finished kibble is smaller than its position suggests.

Ingredient splitting

A recipe can list several forms of the same plant, for example "peas, pea protein, pea fiber", or "brewers rice, rice flour, brown rice". Each form weighs less than the total, so each lands lower on the list. Added together, they might outweigh the meat at the top. This is legal; it just means you should look for related ingredients and mentally group them.

What the first ingredient can tell you

  • Which protein the recipe is built around, if it's a named meat or meal.
  • Whether fresh meat or a meal is used.

What it can't tell you

  • How much of each ingredient is in the food.
  • The nutrient content; that's in the guaranteed analysis.
  • Whether other proteins are present further down. A "salmon" food listing salmon first can still contain chicken fat or egg.

The product name helps too

AAFCO naming rules link the name to amounts. A product called "Chicken Dog Food" must be at least 95% chicken excluding added water; "Chicken Dinner" at least 25%; "with chicken" at least 3%. EU labels often state percentages next to named ingredients. See how to read pet food labels.
