Can pet food contain ingredients that aren't on the label?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Yes. Studies testing pet foods found undeclared animal or plant ingredients in many products, including some sold for elimination diets.

On this page

  2. What it means for you
  3. Questions to ask a manufacturer
  4. How Pawfood shows this
  5. Sources

Yes. Studies that tested commercial pet foods with DNA or protein methods found ingredients that were not on the label in a median of 45% of the foods tested, ranging from 0% to 83% depending on the study (Olivry and Mueller 2018). This was also found in foods sold as "novel protein" or "limited ingredient". Whether those traces lead to reactions in allergic pets hasn't been established.

What the studies found

A 2018 review pooled 17 studies and one abstract:

  • Undeclared ingredients were detected in 0–83% of tested diets (median 45%), and in 33–83% of foods with "novel" or "limited" ingredients.
  • Missing ingredients (listed but not found) were less common: 0–38% (median 1%).
  • Hydrolysed diets were rarely mislabelled; only one possible case was found among those tested.

Sources of undeclared material can include shared equipment, raw material supply and residues between production runs. Labels don't show cross-contact.

What it means for you

  • A label is the best information you have, but not a complete guarantee of what's in the bag.
  • For most pets this doesn't matter. For a pet in a strict elimination trial, or with a confirmed severe allergy, it can.
  • That's one reason vets often choose veterinary diets, especially hydrolysed ones, or home-prepared recipes for diet trials.

Questions to ask a manufacturer

  1. Is this product made on lines shared with other proteins? Which ones?
  2. How are lines cleaned between products?
  3. Do you test finished products for undeclared species?

Write down the date and answer, and the lot number of the bag.

How Pawfood shows this

Pawfood checks what's printed on the label against your pet's list. It can't detect anything that isn't listed. That's why "No recorded match" never means the food is allergen-free, and why Pawfood reminds you that labels can be incomplete and shared equipment isn't listed. If your pet reacts to a food with no recorded match, talk to your vet.
