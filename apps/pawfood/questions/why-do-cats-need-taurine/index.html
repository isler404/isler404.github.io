What is taurine and why do cats need it?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

Taurine is an amino acid cats can't make in sufficient amounts, so it must come from food. Low taurine has been linked to heart and eye disease in cats.

Taurine is an amino acid (strictly, an amino sulfonic acid) found mainly in animal tissue. Cats can't make enough of it themselves, so it has to come from their diet. In the 1970s and 1980s, research linked low taurine in cats to retinal degeneration and to a form of heart disease, dilated cardiomyopathy, which is why complete cat foods are formulated to supply it.

Why cats are different

Most mammals, including dogs, can build taurine from other sulfur-containing amino acids. Cats have limited ability to do this, and they also use taurine to process bile acids, losing some of it constantly. So they depend on a steady supply in their food.

What the research found

  • Eyes: cats fed a casein-based diet developed degeneration of the retina, associated with low taurine (Hayes, Carey and Schmidt 1975).
  • Heart: low blood taurine was found in cats with dilated cardiomyopathy eating commercial foods; taurine supplementation was associated with recovery of heart function (Pion and colleagues 1987).

After these findings, manufacturers and nutrient guidelines adjusted taurine levels in cat food. AAFCO cat nutrient profiles and FEDIAF guidelines both include taurine minimums for cats.

Where to find it on a label

Meat, fish and organs contain taurine naturally, but processing and cooking reduce it, so most complete cat foods also list taurine in the vitamin and mineral section. A complete and balanced cat food is formulated to meet the taurine requirement for the stated life stage. Treats, toppers and complementary foods don't have to.

What about dogs?

Dogs normally make their own taurine. Taurine came up in the U.S. FDA's investigation of dilated cardiomyopathy in dogs eating certain diets, but the FDA has not established a link to specific products. Questions about a particular dog belong with your vet.

Home-made and vegetarian diets

Diets prepared at home, or plant-based diets, need careful formulation to supply enough taurine for a cat. Ask your vet or a veterinary nutritionist before feeding one.

How Pawfood shows this

When you scan a food for a cat, Pawfood adds a note if the ingredient list doesn't mention added taurine. That note isn't a judgement: taurine may come from the meat itself, and the adequacy statement tells you whether the food is designed to be complete. Pawfood doesn't measure nutrient levels.
