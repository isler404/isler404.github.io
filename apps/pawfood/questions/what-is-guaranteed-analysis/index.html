What does the guaranteed analysis on pet food mean?

Last updated 12 November 2026 · By Pawfood

It lists minimum crude protein and fat and maximum fiber and moisture, as fed. They're limits, not exact amounts, and need converting to compare foods.

The guaranteed analysis is the nutrient panel on a pet food label. In the US it must state at least four values: minimum crude protein, minimum crude fat, maximum crude fiber and maximum moisture. They are limits the manufacturer guarantees, not exact amounts, and they include the food's water, so wet and dry foods can't be compared directly.

The four required values

ValueTypeWhat it means
Crude proteinminimumAt least this much protein
Crude fatminimumAt least this much fat
Crude fibermaximumNo more than this much fiber
MoisturemaximumNo more than this much water

"Crude" refers to the laboratory method, not to the quality of the nutrient. Crude protein is estimated from nitrogen content, and crude fiber measures only part of the total fiber.

Many labels add more guarantees, such as ash, calcium, phosphorus, taurine or omega-3 fatty acids. If ash is listed, it is a maximum.

EU labels

In the EU, the same panel is called analytical constituents and lists protein, fat (fat content), crude fibre and crude ash, plus moisture when it exceeds 14%. The values are declared amounts with permitted tolerances rather than minimums and maximums.

Why you can't compare as printed

The values are as fed, water included. A wet food with 78% moisture will always look low in protein next to a kibble with 10% moisture. Convert both to a dry matter basis first, or compare per 1,000 kcal.

What it doesn't tell you

  • Where the protein comes from. Animal and plant proteins both count.
  • Digestibility. Two foods with the same numbers can be digested differently.
  • Carbohydrates. Rarely listed; they can only be estimated when moisture, protein, fat, fiber and ash are all stated.
  • Exact amounts. Real protein and fat are usually somewhat above the minimum.
